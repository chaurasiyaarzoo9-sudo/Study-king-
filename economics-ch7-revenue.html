Chapter 7 — Revenue

Revenue refers to the receipts or income earned by a firm from selling its goods and services.

Chapter Roadmap:
Revenue → Total Revenue → Average Revenue → Marginal Revenue → Formulas → Schedules → TR Graph → AR & MR Graph → Perfect Competition → Numericals → Exam Questions

1. Meaning of Revenue

Revenue is the money received by a firm from the sale of its output during a particular period.

For example, if a firm sells 100 units of a commodity at ₹20 per unit, its total revenue is ₹2,000.

2. Types of Revenue

  1. Total Revenue (TR)
  2. Average Revenue (AR)
  3. Marginal Revenue (MR)
These three concepts are closely related and are important for understanding a firm's revenue behaviour.

3. Total Revenue (TR)

Total Revenue is the total receipts obtained by a firm from the sale of its total output.

TR = Price × Quantity Sold
TR = P × Q

Example

If price = ₹10 and quantity sold = 50 units:

TR = ₹10 × 50 = ₹500

4. Average Revenue (AR)

Average Revenue is revenue earned per unit of output sold.

AR = TR ÷ Q

Since:

TR = P × Q

Therefore:

AR = P
Important: Average Revenue is equal to price.

5. Marginal Revenue (MR)

Marginal Revenue is the addition to total revenue resulting from the sale of one additional unit of output.

MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ

Where:

  • ΔTR = Change in Total Revenue
  • ΔQ = Change in Quantity Sold
MR measures the change in total revenue when one more unit is sold.

7. Relationship Between TR and MR

  • When MR is positive, TR increases.
  • When MR is decreasing but positive, TR increases at a decreasing rate.
  • When MR is zero, TR is maximum.
  • When MR is negative, TR falls.
MR = 0 → TR is maximum
MR > 0 → TR rises
MR < 0 → TR falls

8. Total Revenue Curve

TR Curve
TR rises while MR is positive. TR is maximum when MR becomes zero.

9. Average Revenue and Marginal Revenue

Under imperfect competition, a firm generally faces a downward sloping demand curve. Since AR is equal to price, the AR curve also slopes downward.

MR lies below AR because the firm has to reduce price to sell additional units in this situation.

10. AR and MR Curves

AR and MR Curves
Under imperfect competition, MR lies below AR.

11. Revenue Under Perfect Competition

Under perfect competition, the firm is a price taker. The market determines the price and an individual firm accepts that price.

Therefore, the price remains constant for the individual firm.

AR = MR = Price

12. AR and MR Under Perfect Competition

AR = MR = Price
For a perfectly competitive firm, AR and MR are represented by the same horizontal line at the market price.

13. Relationship Between AR and Price

Average Revenue is equal to price because:

AR = TR ÷ Q

Since:

TR = P × Q

Therefore:

AR = (P × Q) ÷ Q = P
Hence, AR curve is the firm's demand curve.

14. Revenue and Elasticity of Demand

The relationship between revenue and price elasticity of demand is useful for understanding how changes in price affect total revenue.

Elastic Demand

When demand is elastic, a fall in price can increase total revenue, all else equal.

Inelastic Demand

When demand is inelastic, a fall in price can reduce total revenue, all else equal.

Unitary Elasticity

At unitary elasticity, a proportional change in price and quantity can leave total revenue unchanged.

These relationships assume the relevant demand curve and other conditions remain unchanged.

15. Solved Numerical — TR

A firm sells 50 units at ₹20 per unit. Find TR.

TR = P × Q

TR = 20 × 50

TR = ₹1,000

16. Solved Numerical — AR

A firm's TR is ₹800 and quantity sold is 40 units. Find AR.

AR = TR ÷ Q

AR = 800 ÷ 40

AR = ₹20
Since AR = Price, the price is also ₹20.

17. Solved Numerical — MR

TR increases from ₹500 to ₹560 when quantity increases from 10 units to 11 units. Find MR.

MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ

MR = (560 − 500) ÷ (11 − 10)

MR = ₹60

18. Solved Numerical — Find TR from AR

AR is ₹25 and quantity sold is 30 units. Find TR.

TR = AR × Q

TR = 25 × 30

TR = ₹750

19. Solved Numerical — Find MR from TR Schedule

TR at 4 units is ₹100 and TR at 5 units is ₹120. Find MR of the 5th unit.

MR = TR₅ − TR₄

MR = 120 − 100

MR = ₹20

23. Quick Concept Check

Q1. If MR is positive, what happens to TR?

Answer: TR increases.

Q2. When is TR maximum?

Answer: When MR becomes zero.

Q3. What is AR equal to?

Answer: Price.

Q4. Under perfect competition, what is the relationship between AR and MR?

Answer: AR = MR = Price.

Q5. Under imperfect competition, where does MR lie?

Answer: MR lies below AR.
