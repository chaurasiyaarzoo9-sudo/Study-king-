📖 Chapter 8 — Producer Behaviour and Supply

Producer behaviour studies how a producer decides the quantity of goods and services to produce by using available factors of production. It also studies production, costs, revenue and supply.

Chapter Roadmap:
Production → Production Function → TP → AP → MP → Returns to Factor → Cost → Fixed Cost → Variable Cost → TC → AC → MC → Revenue → Supply → Supply Schedule → Supply Curve → Determinants of Supply → Elasticity of Supply → Important Questions

1. Who is a Producer?

A producer is an individual or organisation that combines different factors of production to produce goods and services.

Example:
A bakery uses flour, labour, machines, electricity and premises to produce bread and cakes. The bakery is a producer.

2. Factors of Production

Factor Meaning Example
Land Natural resources Land, water, minerals
Labour Human physical and mental effort Workers
Capital Man-made resources used in production Machines, tools
Entrepreneur Organises production and bears risk Business owner

3. Production

Production is the process of transforming inputs into output. Inputs are the resources used in production and output is the quantity of goods or services produced.

Inputs → Production Process → Output
Example:
Milk + Labour + Machine + Electricity → Processing → Packaged Milk

4. Production Function

A production function shows the relationship between inputs used and maximum output that can be produced with given technology.

Q = f(L, K)

Where:

  • Q = Output
  • L = Labour
  • K = Capital
  • f = Functional relationship
Production function depends on the state of technology.

5. Short Run and Long Run

Short Run Long Run
At least one factor is fixed All factors can be varied
Some inputs cannot be changed immediately Firm can adjust all inputs
Example: factory size fixed Factory size can also be changed
A factory may increase workers quickly in the short run, but changing the size of the factory may require more time.

6. Total Product (TP)

Total Product is the total quantity of output produced by all units of a variable factor, keeping other factors constant.

TP = Total Output
If 5 workers together produce 100 units, then TP = 100 units.

7. Average Product (AP)

Average Product is output per unit of the variable factor.

AP = TP ÷ L

Where L represents units of labour.

8. Marginal Product (MP)

Marginal Product is the change in total product resulting from using one additional unit of the variable factor.

MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL
If TP increases from 50 to 65 when one additional worker is hired:

MP = 65 − 50 = 15 units

10. Important Relationship Between TP and MP

  • When MP is positive, TP increases.
  • When MP becomes zero, TP reaches its maximum.
  • When MP becomes negative, TP falls.
Remember:
MP > 0 → TP rises
MP = 0 → TP maximum
MP < 0 → TP falls

11. Relationship Between AP and MP

  • When MP > AP, AP rises.
  • When MP = AP, AP is maximum.
  • When MP < AP, AP falls.
Golden Rule:
MP cuts AP from above at the maximum point of AP.

12. Law of Variable Proportions

The Law of Variable Proportions explains how output changes when more units of a variable factor are combined with fixed factors, while technology remains unchanged.

It is also known as the Law of Diminishing Returns in its later stage.

Three Stages

  1. Increasing Returns to a Factor
  2. Diminishing Returns to a Factor
  3. Negative Returns to a Factor

13. Stage I — Increasing Returns

In the first stage, the variable factor is increased and TP rises at an increasing rate initially. MP rises and later begins to fall. AP continues to rise up to its maximum.

Stage I ends when AP reaches its maximum and MP becomes equal to AP.

14. Stage II — Diminishing Returns

In Stage II, TP continues to increase but at a diminishing rate. MP is positive but falling.

Stage II ends when MP becomes zero and TP reaches its maximum.

15. Stage III — Negative Returns

In Stage III, adding more units of the variable factor causes TP to decrease and MP becomes negative.

A rational producer generally does not operate in Stage III because additional variable input reduces total output.

17. Cost of Production

Cost of production refers to the expenditure incurred by a producer on factors of production and other inputs used to produce goods and services.

Main cost concepts: TFC, TVC, TC, AFC, AVC, AC and MC.

18. Total Fixed Cost (TFC)

Total Fixed Cost is the cost that does not change with the level of output in the short run.

Examples: rent of factory, fixed salaries, insurance premium and certain administrative expenses.
TFC remains constant as output changes.

19. Total Variable Cost (TVC)

Total Variable Cost changes with the level of output.

Examples: raw materials, fuel used in production and some forms of labour cost.

20. Total Cost (TC)

TC = TFC + TVC
If TFC = ₹500 and TVC = ₹1,200:

TC = ₹500 + ₹1,200

TC = ₹1,700

21. Average Fixed Cost (AFC)

AFC = TFC ÷ Q

AFC falls continuously as output increases because the fixed cost is spread over more units.

22. Average Variable Cost (AVC)

AVC = TVC ÷ Q

AVC generally follows a U-shaped curve in the standard short-run cost model.

23. Average Cost (AC)

AC = TC ÷ Q

Since TC = TFC + TVC:

AC = AFC + AVC

24. Marginal Cost (MC)

Marginal Cost is the change in total cost caused by producing one additional unit of output.

MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ

Because fixed cost does not change with output:

MC = ΔTVC ÷ ΔQ

26. Important Cost Relationships

TC: TFC + TVC

AC: AFC + AVC

AFC: TFC ÷ Q

AVC: TVC ÷ Q

27. Revenue

Revenue is the money received by a producer from the sale of goods and services.

TR = Price × Quantity
AR = TR ÷ Q
MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ

28. Supply

Supply refers to the quantity of a commodity that a seller is willing and able to sell at different possible prices during a given period, other things remaining constant.

Supply is not just the desire to sell. The producer must be willing and able to sell.

29. Individual Supply and Market Supply

Individual Supply

Quantity supplied by a single producer at different prices.

Market Supply

Total quantity supplied by all producers in the market at different prices.

Market Supply = Sum of individual supplies

30. Law of Supply

Other things remaining constant, quantity supplied generally increases with an increase in price and decreases with a decrease in price.

Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑
Price ↓ → Quantity Supplied ↓

32. Supply Curve

The supply curve generally slopes upward from left to right, showing the positive relationship between price and quantity supplied.

33. Determinants of Supply

  • Price of the commodity
  • Prices of inputs
  • Technology
  • Prices of related goods
  • Taxes and subsidies
  • Number of firms
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Natural factors

34. Change in Quantity Supplied vs Change in Supply

Change in Quantity Supplied Change in Supply
Caused by change in own price Caused by factors other than own price
Movement along same supply curve Shift of supply curve
Extension or contraction Increase or decrease in supply

35. Extension and Contraction of Supply

Extension of Supply

When price increases, quantity supplied increases, causing an upward movement along the same supply curve.

Contraction of Supply

When price decreases, quantity supplied decreases, causing a downward movement along the same supply curve.

36. Increase and Decrease in Supply

Increase in Supply

At the same price, producers supply a greater quantity. The supply curve shifts to the right.

Decrease in Supply

At the same price, producers supply a smaller quantity. The supply curve shifts to the left.

37. Causes of Increase in Supply

  • Improvement in technology
  • Decrease in input prices
  • Increase in subsidies
  • Decrease in taxes
  • Increase in number of firms
  • Favourable natural conditions

38. Causes of Decrease in Supply

  • Increase in input prices
  • Higher taxes
  • Reduction in subsidies
  • Adverse natural conditions
  • Increase in production costs
  • Decrease in number of firms

39. Elasticity of Supply

Elasticity of supply measures the responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price.

Es = Percentage Change in Quantity Supplied ÷ Percentage Change in Price

40. Types of Elasticity of Supply

  • Perfectly inelastic supply
  • Relatively inelastic supply
  • Unitary elastic supply
  • Relatively elastic supply
  • Perfectly elastic supply
The value of elasticity indicates how strongly quantity supplied responds to a change in price.

41. Numerical — Average Product

A producer employs 5 workers and produces 100 units. Find Average Product.

AP = TP ÷ L

AP = 100 ÷ 5

AP = 20 units per worker

42. Numerical — Marginal Product

Total product increases from 80 units to 95 units when labour increases from 4 to 5 workers. Find MP.

MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL

= (95 − 80) ÷ (5 − 4)

MP = 15 units

43. Numerical — Total Cost

TFC = ₹500 and TVC = ₹1,500. Find TC.

TC = TFC + TVC

= 500 + 1500

TC = ₹2,000

44. Numerical — Average Cost

Total cost is ₹2,000 and output is 100 units. Find average cost.

AC = TC ÷ Q

= 2000 ÷ 100

AC = ₹20

45. Numerical — Elasticity of Supply

Suppose quantity supplied rises by 20% when price rises by 10%. Find elasticity of supply.

Es = % Change in Quantity Supplied ÷ % Change in Price

= 20 ÷ 10

Es = 2
