Chapter 2 — Consumer's Equilibrium

Consumer's equilibrium explains how a rational consumer chooses different quantities of goods to obtain maximum satisfaction from limited income.

Main approaches:
1. Utility Analysis
2. Indifference Curve Analysis

1. Utility

Utility refers to the want-satisfying capacity of a commodity. It is the satisfaction a consumer derives from consuming a good or service.

Total Utility (TU)

Total Utility is the total satisfaction obtained from consuming all units of a commodity.

Marginal Utility (MU)

Marginal Utility is the change in Total Utility resulting from consuming one additional unit.

MU = Change in TU ÷ Change in Quantity

Graph: TU and Quantity Consumed

4. Marginal Utility (MU) Curve

Graph: MU and Quantity Consumed
Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility: With successive consumption, MU generally decreases, other things remaining constant.

5. Relationship Between TU and MU

  • When MU is positive, TU increases.
  • When MU decreases but remains positive, TU increases at a decreasing rate.
  • When MU becomes zero, TU is maximum.
  • When MU becomes negative, TU decreases.
TU is maximum when MU = 0

6. Consumer's Equilibrium — Single Commodity

A consumer reaches equilibrium when the marginal utility obtained from the commodity is equal to its price, assuming utility is measured in monetary terms.

MU = Price

Equilibrium condition

  1. MU should be equal to price.
  2. MU should be decreasing.

7. Consumer's Equilibrium — Two Commodities

When a consumer consumes two commodities X and Y, equilibrium is achieved when marginal utility per rupee spent on both commodities is equal.

MUx / Px = MUy / Py

At equilibrium, the consumer allocates expenditure so that the marginal utility per rupee from the goods is equal, subject to the budget constraint.

8. Budget Line

A budget line shows all combinations of two goods that a consumer can purchase with a given income and given prices.

PxX + PyY = M
Budget Line
Every point on the budget line represents a combination that completely uses the consumer's income.

9. Indifference Curve

An indifference curve represents different combinations of two goods that provide the consumer with the same level of satisfaction.

Indifference Curve
A higher indifference curve generally represents a higher level of satisfaction, assuming the usual preferences.

10. Consumer's Equilibrium — Indifference Curve Approach

Under the indifference curve approach, consumer equilibrium is achieved at the point where the budget line is tangent to the highest attainable indifference curve.

MRSxy = Px / Py
Consumer Equilibrium: Budget Line and Indifference Curve
Equilibrium point E: The budget line is tangent to the highest attainable indifference curve at the consumer's equilibrium.

11. Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)

MRS is the rate at which a consumer is willing to sacrifice units of one commodity to obtain an additional unit of another commodity, while keeping satisfaction constant.

MRSxy = ΔY / ΔX

As the consumer moves down a conventional indifference curve, MRS generally diminishes.

12. Important Assumptions

  • The consumer is rational.
  • Income is limited.
  • Prices of goods are given.
  • The consumer has preferences.
  • The consumer aims to maximise satisfaction.
  • Goods are assumed to be divisible for analytical purposes.
