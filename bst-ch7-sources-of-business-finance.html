📚 Chapter 7 — Introduction

Every business needs money to start, operate and expand. Finance is required for purchasing assets, buying materials, paying employees, meeting day-to-day expenses and financing growth.

Simple Idea:

Business Need ↓
Finance ↓
Business Operations + Growth
Example:
A new business may need money to purchase machinery, rent premises, buy raw materials and pay employees.

1. Meaning of Business Finance ⭐

Business finance refers to the funds required by a business for carrying out its various activities.

In simple words:

Business Finance = Money required for business activities.

2. Why Does a Business Need Finance?

1. Fixed Capital Requirement

Money is required for acquiring long-term assets such as land, buildings, machinery and equipment.

2. Working Capital Requirement

Funds are required for day-to-day activities such as purchasing materials, paying wages and meeting operating expenses.

3. Expansion

Additional finance may be required when a business expands its production capacity or enters new markets.

4. Modernisation

Businesses may need funds to replace old equipment with newer technology.

5. Other Business Needs

Finance may also be required for research, marketing, emergencies and other business activities.

4. Owners' Funds

Owners' funds are funds contributed by the owners of the business or generated and retained in the business.

Main Sources

  • Equity Shares
  • Retained Earnings

5. Equity Shares ⭐

Equity shares represent ownership capital of a company. Equity shareholders are the real owners of the company and generally have voting rights subject to applicable law and company provisions.

Example:
A company issues equity shares to raise long-term capital from investors.

Features

  • Equity shareholders are owners of the company.
  • Dividend is not a fixed obligation.
  • Equity shareholders generally have voting rights.
  • They bear business risk.
  • Equity capital is generally a permanent source of finance.

6. Retained Earnings

Retained earnings are the portion of profit retained in the business instead of being distributed completely to shareholders.

Example:
If a company earns profit and keeps part of it in the business for future expansion, that amount is retained earnings.

Advantages

  • No flotation costs.
  • No repayment obligation like a loan.
  • Can strengthen the financial position of the business.
  • Useful for expansion and modernisation.

7. Borrowed Funds

Borrowed funds are funds obtained from outside parties and generally have to be repaid according to agreed terms.

Main Sources

  • Debentures
  • Loans from financial institutions
  • Public deposits
  • Commercial banks
  • Trade credit

8. Debentures ⭐

Debentures are instruments acknowledging a company's debt. Debenture holders are creditors of the company, not owners.

Important:

Equity Shareholder = Owner
Debenture Holder = Creditor

Features

  • Debenture holders are creditors.
  • Interest is generally payable according to the terms of issue.
  • Debentures have a specified maturity or redemption arrangement, subject to their terms.
  • They do not generally carry voting rights.

9. Loans from Commercial Banks

Commercial banks provide loans and other credit facilities to businesses for working capital and other requirements.

Example:
A business takes a bank loan to purchase machinery or meet working capital requirements.

Advantages

  • Quick access to finance subject to bank approval.
  • Useful for short-term and medium-term requirements.
  • Interest and repayment terms are generally agreed in advance.

10. Loans from Financial Institutions

Financial institutions can provide medium and long-term finance for business activities such as expansion and modernisation.

Example:
A company obtains a long-term loan from a financial institution to expand its production capacity.

11. Public Deposits

Public deposits are funds raised directly by companies from members of the public for a specified period, subject to applicable rules and regulations.

Example:
A company invites eligible investors to deposit money with it for a specified period at an agreed rate of interest.

Advantages

  • Can provide an alternative source of borrowed funds.
  • May reduce dependence on banks.
  • Can be useful for medium-term financing.

12. Trade Credit ⭐

Trade credit is credit extended by suppliers when goods or services are purchased on credit and payment is made later.

Example:
A retailer purchases goods from a wholesaler and agrees to pay after 30 days.
Supplier → Goods Now
Business → Payment Later

13. Commercial Paper

Commercial paper is a short-term unsecured money market instrument issued by eligible companies to raise short-term funds, subject to applicable regulations.

Features

  • Short-term source of finance.
  • Generally unsecured.
  • Used by financially sound companies.
  • Issued according to applicable regulatory requirements.

14. Factoring

Factoring is a financial arrangement in which a business transfers its receivables or invoices to a factor, usually for immediate funds and collection services, subject to the agreement.

Example:
A company has outstanding customer invoices. It uses a factoring arrangement to obtain funds against those receivables before the customers make payment.

15. Lease Financing

Lease financing allows a business to use an asset for a specified period in return for periodic payments, according to the lease agreement.

Example:
A business leases equipment instead of purchasing it immediately.

16. International Sources of Finance 🌍

Businesses may also raise funds from international financial markets and institutions, subject to applicable laws and regulations.

Examples

  • Foreign currency loans
  • International financial institutions
  • Depository receipts
  • International capital markets

17. Depository Receipts

ADR — American Depository Receipt

An ADR represents shares of a non-US company and is traded in the United States through a depository arrangement.

GDR — Global Depository Receipt

A GDR represents shares of a company and can be traded in international markets through a depository arrangement.

Basis ADR GDR
Full Form American Depository Receipt Global Depository Receipt
Market United States International markets
Purpose Access to US investors/capital market Access to international investors/capital markets

18. Owners' Funds vs Borrowed Funds ⭐

20. Factors Affecting Choice of Source of Finance

1. Cost

The business considers the cost of raising and using finance.

2. Financial Strength

The financial position of the business affects the availability of different sources.

3. Purpose and Period

Short-term needs and long-term needs may require different sources.

4. Risk

The business considers repayment obligations and financial risk.

5. Control

Some sources may affect ownership or control, while debt generally does not create ownership rights.

6. Flexibility

The business may prefer a source that provides suitable flexibility according to its requirements.

7. Effect on Creditworthiness

The level and type of borrowing can affect the financial profile and creditworthiness of the business.

21. Case Study Practice 📝

Case 1:

A company issues shares to investors to raise long-term capital.

Question: Identify the source.

Answer: Equity Shares.
Case 2:

A company keeps part of its profit in the business instead of distributing the entire amount.

Question: Identify the source.

Answer: Retained Earnings.
Case 3:

A retailer purchases goods today and agrees to pay the supplier after 30 days.

Question: Identify the source.

Answer: Trade Credit.
Case 4:

A company borrows money from a bank to meet its working capital requirements.

Question: Identify the source.

Answer: Bank Finance / Bank Loan.
Case 5:

A company raises short-term funds by issuing an eligible unsecured money market instrument.

Question: Identify the source.

Answer: Commercial Paper.
