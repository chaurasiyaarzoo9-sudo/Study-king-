📚 Chapter 4 — Business Services

Services are activities or benefits provided to satisfy the needs of people and businesses. Unlike physical goods, services are generally intangible and are consumed as they are provided.

Simple Meaning:

Goods → Physical products

Services → Activities/benefits provided to satisfy needs
Examples:
Banking, insurance, transportation, warehousing and communication are important examples of business services.

1. Characteristics of Services ⭐

1. Intangibility

Services cannot normally be seen, touched or physically possessed before purchase.

Example: You can see a bank building, but you cannot physically touch the banking service itself.

2. Inconsistency / Variability

The quality of a service may vary depending upon the person providing the service, time and circumstances.

Example: The experience of customer service may differ between employees or different occasions.

3. Inseparability

Production and consumption of many services take place at the same time and cannot be completely separated.

Example: A haircut is provided and consumed during the same service interaction.

4. Perishability

Services generally cannot be stored for future use in the same way as physical goods.

Example: An unused hotel room for tonight cannot be stored and sold tomorrow as the same room-night service.

5. Customer Participation

The customer may participate in the delivery or consumption of many services.

Important business services covered in this chapter include:

4. Banking Services 🏦

A bank is a financial institution that accepts deposits from the public and provides various financial services, including lending, payments and other permitted banking activities.

Main Functions of Banks

  • Accepting deposits
  • Providing loans and advances
  • Facilitating payments and transfers
  • Providing remittance facilities
  • Providing other banking and financial services

5. Types of Bank Deposits ⭐

1. Savings Account

A savings account is generally designed for individuals to save money while allowing withdrawals according to the account rules.

Example: A student may use a savings account for regular deposits and payments.

2. Current Account

Current accounts are generally used by businesses and allow frequent transactions subject to the bank's terms.

3. Fixed Deposit Account

Money is deposited for a specified period at an agreed rate of interest, subject to the applicable terms.

4. Recurring Deposit Account

A fixed amount is deposited regularly for a specified period.

6. Important Banking Services

  • Cheque facilities
  • Demand drafts and other payment instruments
  • Electronic fund transfer facilities
  • Internet banking
  • Mobile banking
  • ATM services
  • Debit card services
  • Collection of cheques
  • Remittance services

7. Insurance 🛡️

Insurance is a mechanism for managing specified risks by transferring the financial consequences of covered risks to an insurer in return for a premium, subject to the terms and conditions of the policy.

Basic idea:

Risk → Insurance contract → Premium → Financial protection according to policy terms

8. Principles of Insurance ⭐⭐⭐

1. Principle of Utmost Good Faith

Both parties are expected to disclose material facts honestly and fully as required by the insurance contract.

Example: A proposer should disclose relevant material information asked for in the insurance proposal.

2. Principle of Insurable Interest

The insured must have a legally recognised financial or other interest in the subject matter of insurance, as applicable to the type of insurance.

3. Principle of Indemnity

For contracts to which the principle applies, insurance aims to compensate for the covered financial loss rather than provide an unjustified gain.

4. Principle of Contribution

Where the same risk is insured with more than one insurer under applicable conditions, insurers may contribute towards the covered loss according to the relevant rules.

5. Principle of Subrogation

After paying a covered claim, the insurer may acquire the insured's rights against a responsible third party to the extent permitted by law and the policy.

6. Principle of Causa Proxima

The proximate or dominant cause of a loss is considered while determining whether the loss is covered, subject to policy terms.

7. Principle of Mitigation of Loss

The insured should take reasonable steps to minimise the loss after the occurrence of an insured event.

9. Insurance Principles — Quick Revision

Principle Simple Meaning
Utmost Good Faith Disclose material facts honestly
Insurable Interest Financial/legal interest in subject matter
Indemnity Compensation for covered loss, where applicable
Contribution Insurers share covered loss where applicable
Subrogation Insurer may obtain rights against responsible third party after claim payment
Causa Proxima Consider the proximate cause of loss
Mitigation of Loss Take reasonable steps to reduce loss

10. Types of Insurance

Life Insurance

Life insurance provides financial protection according to the terms of the policy in relation to specified life-related events.

Fire Insurance

Fire insurance provides cover against specified losses caused by fire and related insured risks according to policy terms.

Marine Insurance

Marine insurance provides protection against specified risks related to marine transport, cargo or other insured marine interests.

11. Communication Services 📱

Communication services help businesses exchange information and messages quickly and efficiently.

Examples

  • Postal services
  • Telecommunication
  • Internet services
  • Email
  • Mobile communication

12. Transportation Services 🚚

Transportation involves the movement of people and goods from one place to another.

Example:
A manufacturer may use road, rail, air or water transport to move goods from the production location to markets.

Importance

  • Connects producers with markets.
  • Moves raw materials to production centres.
  • Moves finished goods to consumers.
  • Supports large-scale business operations.
  • Helps overcome geographical distance.

13. Warehousing 📦

Warehousing means storing goods safely until they are required for production, distribution or consumption.

Functions of Warehousing

  • Storage of goods
  • Protection of goods
  • Regular supply of goods
  • Price stabilisation support
  • Financing support through warehouse receipts where applicable
  • Grading and packaging services in some warehouses

14. Types of Warehouses

Type Meaning
Private Warehouse Owned or controlled by a private business for its own storage needs.
Public Warehouse Provides storage facilities to businesses on payment of charges.
Bonded Warehouse Stores imported goods on which customs duties are yet to be paid, subject to customs rules.
Government Warehouse Owned or controlled by government agencies for specified storage purposes.
Co-operative Warehouse Storage facility operated by a co-operative organisation for its members or users.

16. Goods vs Services

Basis Goods Services
Nature Generally tangible Generally intangible
Storage Can generally be stored Generally cannot be stored in the same way
Production & Consumption Can often be separated Often closely connected
Ownership Ownership can generally be transferred Service does not usually involve transfer of ownership
Quality Can often be standardised physically May vary depending on delivery

17. Case Study Practice 📝

Case 1:

Rahul deposits money in a bank and uses internet banking to transfer money to a supplier.

Question: Which business service is involved?

Answer: Banking service.
Case 2:

A business purchases insurance for its factory against specified fire risks.

Question: Which principle requires the relevant material facts to be disclosed honestly?

Answer: Principle of Utmost Good Faith.
Case 3:

A manufacturer stores finished goods in a facility until demand increases.

Question: Which business service is being used?

Answer: Warehousing.
Case 4:

A company transports its finished products from its factory to different markets.

Question: Which service helps overcome geographical distance?

Answer: Transportation.
