Introduction to Accounting | Complete Notes

1. Meaning of Accounting

Accounting is the process of identifying, measuring, recording, classifying, summarising, analysing and communicating financial information to the users of accounting information.

Simple Meaning:
Accounting means systematically recording and summarising the financial transactions of a business and using that information for decision-making.

2. Book-keeping and Accounting

Book-keeping

Book-keeping is mainly concerned with the systematic recording of financial transactions in the books of accounts.

Accounting

Accounting is broader than book-keeping. It includes recording, classifying, summarising, analysing and interpreting financial information.

Basis Book-keeping Accounting
Meaning Recording of transactions Recording, summarising and analysing information
Scope Narrow Wider
Purpose Maintain records Help in decision-making

3. Objectives of Accounting

  • To maintain systematic records of business transactions.
  • To calculate profit or loss.
  • To determine the financial position of the business.
  • To provide useful information to users.
  • To help management in decision-making.
  • To provide information for planning and control.

4. Functions of Accounting

1. Recording

Business transactions are recorded systematically in the books of accounts.

2. Classification

Transactions of similar nature are grouped into appropriate accounts.

3. Summarising

Large amounts of accounting data are summarised through financial statements.

4. Analysis

Financial information is analysed to understand the performance and position of the business.

5. Interpretation

Accounting information is interpreted so that users can make appropriate decisions.

6. Communication

Useful financial information is communicated to interested users.

5. Advantages of Accounting

  • Provides permanent records.
  • Helps calculate profit or loss.
  • Shows financial position.
  • Helps management in decision-making.
  • Helps in comparison of business performance.
  • Provides information to owners and other users.
  • Helps in planning and control.

6. Limitations of Accounting

  • Only monetary transactions are recorded.
  • Accounting is based on certain assumptions and conventions.
  • It may involve estimates.
  • Accounting information may not show the complete picture of a business.
  • Different accounting policies can affect reported figures.

7. Users of Accounting Information

User Why They Need Information
Owners To know profit, loss and financial position.
Management For planning, controlling and decision-making.
Investors To assess investment decisions.
Creditors To assess the ability to repay amounts due.
Banks To evaluate financial position before providing loans.
Government For taxation and regulatory purposes.
Employees To understand stability and performance of the organisation.

8. Basic Accounting Terms

Business Transaction

An economic event involving a business that can be measured in money is called a business transaction.

Example: Goods purchased for ₹10,000 for business purposes.

Capital

Amount invested by the owner in the business is called capital.

Example: Arzoo starts a business with ₹50,000 cash. Here ₹50,000 is capital.

Drawings

Cash or goods withdrawn by the owner from the business for personal use are called drawings.

Assets

Assets are resources owned or controlled by a business that have economic value.

Examples: Cash, Bank balance, Furniture, Building, Machinery, Stock, Debtors.

Liabilities

Liabilities are amounts payable by the business to outsiders.

Examples: Creditors, Bank loan, Outstanding expenses.

Revenue

Revenue is the income generated from the normal activities of a business.

Expense

Expense is the cost incurred to earn revenue.

Income

Income refers to an increase in economic benefits during an accounting period.

Profit

When income is greater than expenses, the difference is profit.

Profit = Revenue − Expenses

Loss

When expenses are greater than revenue, the difference is loss.

Loss = Expenses − Revenue

Goods

Goods are items in which the business deals.

Purchases

Goods bought for resale are called purchases.

Sales

Goods sold by a business are called sales.

Debtor

A person who owes money to the business for goods or services sold on credit is called a debtor.

Creditor

A person to whom the business owes money for goods or services purchased on credit is called a creditor.

10. Accounting Equation

The basic accounting equation expresses the relationship between assets, liabilities and capital.

Assets = Capital + Liabilities

Example

If capital is ₹50,000 and liabilities are ₹20,000:

Assets = ₹50,000 + ₹20,000
Assets = ₹70,000

11. Important Formulae

Profit = Revenue − Expenses
Loss = Expenses − Revenue
Assets = Capital + Liabilities
Capital = Assets − Liabilities
Liabilities = Assets − Capital

12. Quick Revision

  • Accounting records financial transactions.
  • Book-keeping mainly deals with recording transactions.
  • Accounting has a wider scope than book-keeping.
  • Assets are economic resources of a business.
  • Liabilities are obligations payable by the business.
  • Capital represents the owner's investment.
  • Drawings reduce owner's capital.
  • Profit occurs when income exceeds expenses.
  • The basic equation is Assets = Capital + Liabilities.
