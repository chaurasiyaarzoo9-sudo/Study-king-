📚 Chapter 6 — Introduction

A business does not operate in isolation. It uses resources from society, provides goods and services to consumers and interacts with employees, investors, government and the environment.

Therefore, a business is expected to consider the interests of different groups affected by its activities.

Main Idea:

Business → Society → Stakeholders → Responsibilities
Example:
A factory earns profit, but it also needs to treat workers fairly, provide safe products to customers and avoid unnecessary pollution.

1. Meaning of Social Responsibility ⭐

Social responsibility of business refers to the obligation of a business to take decisions and actions that are desirable in terms of the objectives and values of society.

Simple Meaning:

Business should not think only about profit. It should also consider the welfare of society and the interests of its stakeholders.

2. Why Should Business Be Socially Responsible?

1. Justification for Existence and Growth

Business depends on society for resources, customers, employees and other support. Responsible behaviour can help maintain this relationship.

2. Long-Term Interest of Business

Responsible conduct can build goodwill and customer trust, which may support long-term business relationships.

3. Avoidance of Government Regulation

Responsible business practices can reduce the need for excessive regulatory intervention in some areas.

4. Better Public Image

A business that behaves responsibly can develop a positive reputation among customers and other stakeholders.

5. Availability of Resources

Business depends on society for natural, human and financial resources. Responsible use of resources supports sustainable operations.

6. Contribution to Society

Business is part of society and can contribute to solving social and environmental problems within its capabilities.

3. Arguments in Favour of Social Responsibility

  • It improves the public image of business.
  • It can build customer trust and goodwill.
  • It may support long-term business interests.
  • It can help reduce social and environmental problems.
  • It can improve relationships with stakeholders.
  • It can encourage responsible use of resources.

4. Arguments Against Social Responsibility

  • Some argue that social activities may increase business costs.
  • Managers may not always have specialised knowledge of social problems.
  • Different groups may disagree about what is socially desirable.
  • Businesses may have limited resources for social programmes.
Exam Tip ⭐
In an answer, present both sides when the question asks for arguments for and against social responsibility.

5. Responsibility Towards Consumers 🛒

  • Provide good-quality goods and services.
  • Charge reasonable and fair prices.
  • Provide correct information about products.
  • Avoid misleading advertisements.
  • Ensure safety of products.
  • Provide proper after-sales service where applicable.
  • Handle consumer complaints fairly.
  • Avoid unfair trade practices.
Example:
A company should not advertise a product using false claims about its features or performance.

6. Responsibility Towards Employees 👨‍💼

  • Provide fair wages and benefits according to applicable rules.
  • Provide safe and healthy working conditions.
  • Provide appropriate training and development opportunities.
  • Respect employee rights and dignity.
  • Provide fair treatment without unlawful discrimination.
  • Maintain suitable working conditions.

7. Responsibility Towards Owners and Investors 💰

  • Protect the interests of owners and investors.
  • Provide accurate and relevant information.
  • Use business resources efficiently.
  • Maintain proper financial records.
  • Follow applicable laws and governance requirements.

8. Responsibility Towards Government 🏛️

  • Follow laws and regulations.
  • Pay taxes and statutory dues properly.
  • Provide required information to authorities.
  • Cooperate with lawful government requirements.
  • Avoid fraudulent and illegal activities.

9. Responsibility Towards Community 🌱

  • Protect the local environment.
  • Support community development where appropriate.
  • Use resources responsibly.
  • Help reduce harmful effects of business activities.
  • Contribute to social welfare initiatives.

10. Responsibilities Towards Different Groups ⭐

Stakeholder Main Responsibility
Consumers Quality, safety, fair prices and truthful information

Owners/Investors Proper information and efficient use of resources

Community Environmental protection and social welfare

11. Business Responsibility Towards Environment 🌍

Businesses use natural resources and may generate waste or pollution. Therefore, environmental protection is an important area of responsible business behaviour.

Major Environmental Problems

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Land pollution
  • Noise pollution
  • Waste generation
  • Depletion of natural resources

12. Role of Business in Environmental Protection

  • Use cleaner production methods.
  • Reduce unnecessary waste.
  • Recycle and reuse materials where possible.
  • Treat industrial waste properly.
  • Use energy efficiently.
  • Adopt environmentally responsible technology.
  • Follow environmental laws and standards.
Example:
A factory can reduce environmental impact by treating wastewater before discharge and reducing unnecessary emissions.

13. Pollution Control Measures

1. Control at Source

Use cleaner raw materials, improved technology and efficient production methods to reduce pollution at its origin.

2. Treatment of Waste

Waste should be treated appropriately before disposal.

3. Recycling and Reuse

Materials should be reused or recycled wherever practical.

4. Proper Disposal

Waste that cannot be reused should be disposed of using appropriate methods.

14. Meaning of Business Ethics ⭐

Business ethics refers to the moral principles and values that guide the behaviour of a business and its employees.

Simple Meaning:

Business Ethics = Doing business according to accepted moral principles and values.
Example:
A business honestly informs customers about important product limitations instead of deliberately hiding them.

15. Elements of Business Ethics

  • Top management commitment to ethical behaviour.
  • Publication of a code of ethics.
  • Establishment of compliance mechanisms.
  • Involving employees in ethical decision-making.
  • Regular monitoring of ethical practices.
  • Providing suitable ethical training.

16. Role of Top Management in Business Ethics

Top management plays an important role because employees often follow the standards and behaviour demonstrated by senior leaders.

  • Set ethical standards.
  • Communicate ethical expectations.
  • Lead by example.
  • Develop a code of conduct.
  • Monitor compliance.
  • Take appropriate action when ethical standards are violated.

17. Code of Ethics

A code of ethics is a formal statement of the ethical standards, principles and expected behaviour of an organisation.

Possible areas covered:

Honesty • Customer treatment • Employee conduct • Conflict of interest • Confidentiality • Compliance with laws • Fair dealing

20. Case Study Practice 📝

Case 1:

A company discovers that one of its products has an important safety limitation. Instead of hiding the information, it informs customers clearly.

Question: Which concept is reflected?

Answer: Business ethics and responsibility towards consumers.
Case 2:

A factory installs equipment to reduce harmful emissions and treats wastewater before disposal.

Question: Which responsibility is being fulfilled?

Answer: Responsibility towards the environment.
Case 3:

A company provides employees with safe working conditions and appropriate training opportunities.

Question: Towards whom is the business fulfilling its responsibility?

Answer: Employees.
Case 4:

A business deliberately publishes false information about its product to increase sales.

Question: Is this consistent with business ethics?

Answer: No. Deliberately misleading customers is inconsistent with ethical business conduct.
