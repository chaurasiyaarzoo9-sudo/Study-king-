📚 Chapter Overview

Forms of Business Organisation refers to the different legal and organisational structures through which a business can be owned, managed and operated.

Main Forms:

Sole Proprietorship Partnership Hindu Undivided Family Business Cooperative Society Joint Stock Company

1. Sole Proprietorship

Sole proprietorship is a form of business organisation in which the business is owned, managed and controlled by one person.

Example:
A person running a small stationery shop, grocery shop or local repair shop in his/her own name can operate as a sole proprietor.

Main Features

  1. Single ownership
  2. Unlimited liability
  3. Full control
  4. No separate legal entity in the usual sense
  5. Easy formation
  6. Business secrecy
  7. Direct motivation
  8. Quick decision-making

2. Merits of Sole Proprietorship

1. Easy Formation

It is comparatively easy to start because there are fewer formalities than in more complex forms of organisation.

2. Quick Decision-Making

The owner can take decisions without consulting partners or a board of directors.

3. Flexibility

The proprietor can quickly change business policies according to market conditions.

4. Business Secrecy

Important business information can be kept confidential.

5. Direct Incentive

The proprietor generally receives the benefit of business success directly.

6. Personal Contact

The owner can maintain direct relationships with customers and employees.

3. Limitations of Sole Proprietorship

  • Limited financial resources
  • Unlimited liability
  • Limited managerial ability
  • Limited continuity
  • Business may depend heavily on the owner
Important:
Unlimited liability means that the proprietor's personal assets may be at risk for business debts, subject to applicable law.

4. Partnership

Partnership is a form of business organisation in which two or more persons agree to carry on a business and share its profits according to an agreement.

Example:
A, B and C start a business together and agree to share profits according to their partnership agreement.
Important:
The persons who enter into partnership are individually called partners and collectively called the firm.

5. Partnership Deed

A partnership deed is a written agreement containing the terms and conditions governing the relationship between partners.

Usually Contains

  • Name and address of the firm
  • Names and addresses of partners
  • Nature of business
  • Capital contribution
  • Profit-sharing ratio
  • Interest on capital
  • Interest on drawings
  • Salary or commission to partners
  • Rules regarding admission or retirement
  • Settlement of disputes

6. Features of Partnership

1. Two or More Persons

Partnership requires more than one person as partners, subject to the applicable legal limit.

2. Agreement

Partnership arises from an agreement between persons.

3. Lawful Business

The partnership must be formed to carry on a lawful business.

4. Profit Sharing

Partners agree to share profits of the business.

5. Mutual Agency

Each partner may act as an agent of the firm and other partners for business purposes, subject to the law and agreement.

6. Unlimited Liability

Partners generally have unlimited liability for the firm's debts under the applicable partnership law.

7. Types of Partnership

Partnership at Will

A partnership that is formed without specifying a fixed period or a particular undertaking, subject to the applicable law.

Particular Partnership

A partnership formed for a specific undertaking or a specified period.

8. Types of Partners

Type Meaning
Active Partner Takes active part in management and business activities.
Sleeping Partner Invests capital and shares profits but does not actively participate in management.
Nominal Partner Lends name to the firm without normally having a real interest in management.
Partner by Estoppel A person who represents himself/herself as a partner and may become liable to third parties on that representation.
Partner by Holding Out A person who knowingly allows himself/herself to be represented as a partner.

9. Merits of Partnership

  • Easy Formation: Comparatively easy to form.
  • More Resources: More than one person can contribute capital.
  • Flexibility: Partners can make changes according to business needs.
  • Balanced Decisions: Partners can combine their knowledge and experience.
  • Risk Sharing: Business risk is shared among partners.
  • Business Secrecy: Partnership firms are generally not required to disclose as much information as companies.

10. Limitations of Partnership

  • Unlimited liability
  • Limited resources compared with large companies
  • Possibility of conflicts among partners
  • Limited transferability of interest
  • Uncertainty of continuity
  • Mutual agency can create risk for partners

11. Hindu Undivided Family Business (HUF)

A Hindu Undivided Family Business is a form of business organisation based on Hindu law, where the business is owned and managed by members of a Hindu Undivided Family.

The person who manages the family business is traditionally known as the Karta.

12. Features of HUF Business

  • Membership arises by birth in the family, subject to applicable law.
  • Business is managed by the Karta.
  • Other members are commonly referred to as coparceners.
  • Business is governed by applicable Hindu law.
  • Continuity may be maintained despite changes in membership.

13. Merits of HUF Business

  • Easy to become a member by birth, subject to law.
  • Continuity of business.
  • Quick decision-making through the Karta.
  • Family loyalty and cooperation.
  • Business secrecy can be maintained.

14. Limitations of HUF Business

  • Limited managerial ability of the Karta.
  • Limited financial resources.
  • Possibility of family disputes.
  • Limited scope compared with a large company.

15. Cooperative Society

A cooperative society is a voluntary association of persons who come together to promote their common economic interests through mutual cooperation.

Examples:
Consumer cooperative societies, housing cooperatives, producer cooperatives and credit cooperatives.

16. Features of Cooperative Society

  • Voluntary membership
  • Separate legal identity after registration
  • Democratic management
  • Service motive
  • Separate legal existence
  • Limited liability generally applies to members
  • Perpetual succession
  • One member, one vote in the usual cooperative structure

17. Merits of Cooperative Society

  • Easy formation subject to registration requirements
  • Democratic management
  • Limited liability
  • Stable existence
  • Mutual help
  • Protection of weaker sections
  • Service-oriented approach

18. Limitations of Cooperative Society

  • Limited resources
  • Management problems
  • Possibility of internal conflicts
  • Government regulation
  • Limited motivation for members in some situations

19. Joint Stock Company

A company is an association of persons incorporated under company law having a separate legal identity from its members.

Key idea:
A company has a legal identity separate from the persons who own its shares.

20. Features of a Company

1. Separate Legal Entity

The company is legally distinct from its members.

2. Artificial Person

A company is an artificial legal person created by law. It can own property, enter into contracts and sue or be sued in its own name, subject to law.

3. Perpetual Succession

The existence of a company is generally not affected by the death or insolvency of its members.

4. Limited Liability

Members' liability is generally limited according to the form of company and the amount unpaid on shares or the guarantee.

5. Transfer of Shares

Shares of a public company are generally transferable subject to applicable law. Transfer in a private company is restricted according to its articles and applicable law.

6. Common Capital

The capital of a company is divided into shares in a company limited by shares.

21. Private Company

A private company is a company whose articles restrict the transfer of its shares and which is subject to the conditions specified under company law.

Main Characteristics

  • Restriction on transfer of shares
  • Closer ownership structure
  • Generally suitable for comparatively smaller ownership groups
  • Subject to applicable company law requirements

22. Public Company

A public company is a company that is not a private company and is subject to the provisions applicable to public companies under company law.

Public companies can have a wider ownership base and may raise capital from the public subject to applicable legal requirements.

23. Private Company vs Public Company

Basis Private Company Public Company
Transfer of Shares Restricted according to law/articles Generally freely transferable, subject to law
Ownership More closely held Can have wider ownership
Public Issue Cannot invite public to subscribe to its securities May invite public subject to legal requirements
Regulation Subject to company law Subject to company law and additional public-company requirements

24. Company Formation — Basic Stages

  1. Promotion: Business idea is identified and feasibility is considered.
  2. Incorporation: The company is registered under applicable company law.
  3. Capital Subscription: Relevant companies raise capital through the legally permitted process.
The exact legal requirements depend on the type of company and the applicable law.

25. Comparison of Major Forms ⭐

26. Separate Legal Entity

Separate legal entity means that the organisation is legally different from its owners or members.

Example:
If a company enters into a contract, the contract is generally with the company as a separate legal person, rather than with each shareholder personally.

27. Perpetual Succession

Perpetual succession means continuous existence of an organisation despite changes in its members.

Members may change → Organisation continues

28. Artificial Person

An artificial person is a legal person created by law rather than a natural human being.

A company is an example of an artificial legal person.

29. Unlimited Liability

Unlimited liability means that the owner's or partners' liability is not restricted to a fixed amount of business investment, subject to applicable law.

Sole proprietorship and ordinary partnership are commonly associated with unlimited liability.

  • Scale of operations

  • Degree of control desired

  • Need for expansion
