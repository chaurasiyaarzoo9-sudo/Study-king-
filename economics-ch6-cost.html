Chapter 6 — Cost

Cost refers to the expenditure incurred by a producer on the production of goods and services.

Chapter Roadmap:
Meaning of Cost → Fixed Cost → Variable Cost → Total Cost → Average Fixed Cost → Average Variable Cost → Average Cost → Marginal Cost → Schedules → Graphs → Relationships → Numericals → Exam Questions

1. Meaning of Cost

Cost is the expenditure incurred by a firm in producing a commodity or providing a service.

For example, a firm may spend money on raw materials, wages, electricity, rent, transport and machinery.

2. Fixed Cost

Fixed cost is the cost which does not change with the level of output in the short run.

Examples

  • Rent of factory
  • Insurance
  • Permanent salaries
  • Interest on fixed capital
Fixed cost exists even when output is zero.

3. Variable Cost

Variable cost changes with the level of output.

Examples

  • Raw materials
  • Power used in production
  • Casual labour wages
  • Packaging materials
When output is zero, variable cost is generally zero in the short-run production model.

4. Total Fixed Cost (TFC)

Total Fixed Cost is the total cost incurred on fixed factors. It remains constant at different levels of output.

TFC = Constant
Even when output = 0, TFC is positive.

5. Total Variable Cost (TVC)

Total Variable Cost is the total expenditure incurred on variable factors of production.

TVC = Variable Cost per unit × Units of Variable Factor

TVC generally increases as output increases.

6. Total Cost (TC)

Total Cost is the sum of Total Fixed Cost and Total Variable Cost.

TC = TFC + TVC

Important

  • At zero output, TC = TFC.
  • TC and TVC have the same shape but TC starts above TVC.
  • The vertical distance between TC and TVC equals TFC.

7. Average Fixed Cost (AFC)

Average Fixed Cost is fixed cost per unit of output.

AFC = TFC ÷ Q

As output increases, AFC continuously falls.

AFC can never become zero mathematically, although it approaches zero as output becomes very large.

8. Average Variable Cost (AVC)

Average Variable Cost is variable cost per unit of output.

AVC = TVC ÷ Q

AVC generally follows a U-shaped curve in the short run.

9. Average Cost (AC)

Average Cost is the cost per unit of output.

AC = TC ÷ Q

Since TC = TFC + TVC:

AC = AFC + AVC
Average Cost is also called Average Total Cost in many textbooks.

10. Marginal Cost (MC)

Marginal Cost is the addition to total cost caused by producing one additional unit of output.

MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ

Since fixed cost does not change with output:

MC = ΔTVC ÷ ΔQ

12. Relationship Between TC, TFC and TVC

  • TC = TFC + TVC.
  • TFC remains constant.
  • TVC increases with output.
  • TC increases as TVC increases.
  • At zero output, TC = TFC.
  • The vertical gap between TC and TVC remains constant.

13. TC and TVC Curves

Total Cost and Total Variable Cost
The distance between TC and TVC is equal to TFC.

14. TFC Curve

Total Fixed Cost
TFC is a horizontal straight line because it remains constant at all levels of output.

15. TVC Curve

Total Variable Cost

16. AFC Curve

Average Fixed Cost
AFC continuously falls as output increases.

17. AVC and AC Curves

Average Variable Cost and Average Cost
AC lies above AVC because AC = AFC + AVC. The gap between AC and AVC becomes smaller as AFC falls.

18. MC Curve

Marginal Cost
MC is generally U-shaped in the short run under the usual cost structure.

19. Relationship Between MC and AVC

  • When MC is less than AVC, AVC falls.
  • When MC equals AVC, AVC is minimum.
  • When MC is greater than AVC, AVC rises.
MC = AVC → AVC is minimum

20. Relationship Between MC and AC

  • When MC is less than AC, AC falls.
  • When MC equals AC, AC is minimum.
  • When MC is greater than AC, AC rises.
MC = AC → AC is minimum
The MC curve cuts the AC curve at the minimum point of AC. Similarly, MC cuts AVC at the minimum point of AVC.

21. Important Cost Relationships

Condition Effect
MC < AC AC falls
MC = AC AC minimum
MC > AC AC rises
MC < AVC AVC falls
MC = AVC AVC minimum
MC > AVC AVC rises

22. Solved Numerical — TC

A firm's TFC is ₹100 and TVC is ₹250. Find TC.

TC = TFC + TVC

TC = 100 + 250

TC = ₹350

23. Solved Numerical — AFC

TFC is ₹200 and output is 20 units. Find AFC.

AFC = TFC ÷ Q

AFC = 200 ÷ 20

AFC = ₹10

24. Solved Numerical — AVC

TVC is ₹300 and output is 20 units. Find AVC.

AVC = TVC ÷ Q

AVC = 300 ÷ 20

AVC = ₹15

25. Solved Numerical — AC

TC is ₹500 and output is 25 units. Find AC.

AC = TC ÷ Q

AC = 500 ÷ 25

AC = ₹20

26. Solved Numerical — MC

TC rises from ₹300 to ₹350 when output increases from 10 units to 11 units. Find MC.

MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ

MC = (350 − 300) ÷ (11 − 10)

MC = ₹50

27. Solved Numerical — Missing Cost

TC is ₹700 and TFC is ₹200. Find TVC.

TC = TFC + TVC

TVC = TC − TFC

TVC = 700 − 200

TVC = ₹500
