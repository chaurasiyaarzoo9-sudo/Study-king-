📚 Chapter 10 — Introduction

International business refers to business activities that take place across the national boundaries of different countries.

Simple Meaning:

Business within one country = Domestic Business

Business between different countries = International Business
Example:
An Indian company selling its products to customers in Japan is engaged in international business.

1. Features of International Business ⭐

  • Transactions take place across national boundaries.
  • Involves two or more countries.
  • Uses foreign currencies in many transactions.
  • Deals with different legal and regulatory systems.
  • May involve greater geographical distance.
  • Businesses face differences in language and culture.
  • International transportation and documentation may be required.
  • Businesses may face greater uncertainty and risks.

2. Domestic Business vs International Business ⭐

Basis Domestic Business International Business
Area Within one country Across countries
Currency Usually one domestic currency May involve different currencies
Legal System Mainly one country's laws Different countries' laws and regulations
Culture Relatively familiar environment Different cultures and consumer preferences
Distance Generally lower May be significantly greater
Risk Generally fewer international risks May involve additional political, currency and regulatory risks

3. Reasons for International Business

1. Availability of Resources

Countries differ in the availability of natural resources, labour, capital and technology.

2. Specialisation

Countries can specialise in goods and services in which they have relative advantages.

3. Larger Market

International business allows firms to access customers outside their domestic market.

4. Foreign Exchange

International transactions can help businesses and countries earn foreign exchange.

5. Better Use of Capacity

Access to international markets can allow businesses to utilise production capacity more effectively.

Example:
If demand for a company's product is limited in its home country, selling abroad can provide access to additional customers.

Different modes involve different levels of investment, control, risk and commitment.

5. Export Trade

Export means selling goods or services from one country to buyers located in another country.

Example:
An Indian textile company sells garments to a buyer in France. For the Indian company, this is an export transaction.

6. Import Trade

Import means purchasing goods or services from a supplier located in another country.

Example:
An Indian business purchases machinery from Germany. For the Indian business, it is an import.

7. Contract Manufacturing

Contract manufacturing occurs when a business enters into an agreement with a foreign manufacturer to produce goods according to specified requirements.

Advantages

  • May reduce investment in production facilities.
  • Can use the manufacturing expertise of another firm.
  • May allow faster entry into foreign markets.
  • Can help control production costs.
Example:
A foreign brand may enter into an agreement with an Indian manufacturer to produce garments according to the brand's specifications.

8. Licensing

Licensing is an arrangement in which the owner of intellectual property permits a foreign party to use it under agreed terms, usually in return for a fee or royalty.

Intellectual property may include patents, trademarks, technology or other protected rights.

Example:
A company may permit a foreign firm to use its trademark or technology for a specified period under a licensing agreement.

9. Franchising ⭐

Franchising is a contractual arrangement under which the franchisor allows the franchisee to use its brand, business system and other specified rights subject to agreed conditions.

Parties

  • Franchisor: Owner of the business system or brand.
  • Franchisee: Person or business operating under the franchisor's system.
Example:
An international food brand may allow an Indian business operator to run a restaurant using its brand, operating procedures and specified standards.

10. Licensing vs Franchising

Basis Licensing Franchising
Meaning Permission to use specified intellectual property Use of a broader business model/system under agreement
Control Usually more limited Usually greater operational control by franchisor
Use Often technology, patent or trademark Brand, system, procedures and support
Relationship Generally narrower Generally broader business relationship

11. Joint Venture ⭐

A joint venture is a business arrangement in which two or more parties agree to work together on a business activity or project and share resources, risks and returns according to their agreement.

Advantages

  • Sharing of investment.
  • Sharing of risks.
  • Access to local knowledge.
  • Access to technology or expertise.
  • Access to established distribution networks.
Example:
An Indian company and a foreign company may form a joint venture to produce and market a product in India.

12. Wholly Owned Subsidiary

A wholly owned subsidiary is a foreign company controlled through ownership by the parent company, subject to the applicable laws and regulations.

Features

  • High level of ownership and control.
  • Greater investment requirement.
  • Greater exposure to the foreign market.
  • Parent company bears a larger share of the business risk.

14. Benefits of International Business to Firms ⭐

1. Wider Market

Businesses can reach customers in foreign countries.

2. Higher Sales Potential

Access to additional markets can increase sales opportunities.

3. Economies of Scale

Large-scale production may reduce average costs under suitable conditions.

4. Better Capacity Utilisation

International demand can help firms use production capacity more effectively.

5. Access to Technology

International operations can provide access to foreign technology, skills and management practices.

6. Diversification

Operating in multiple markets may diversify a firm's market base.

15. Benefits of International Business to the Country

  • Earns foreign exchange.
  • Promotes economic activity.
  • Encourages specialisation.
  • Provides access to goods and services not produced domestically in sufficient quantity.
  • Can support employment and income generation.
  • May encourage technology and knowledge transfer.

18. Important Documents in International Trade

1. Invoice

Contains details of goods, quantity, price and transaction terms.

2. Bill of Lading

A transport document generally used in sea transportation and serves as evidence of receipt of goods and the contract of carriage.

3. Airway Bill

A transport document used for goods transported by air.

4. Certificate of Origin

Certifies the country in which the goods originated, as required by the relevant authorities or transaction.

5. Insurance Document

Provides evidence of insurance coverage where insurance is taken for the shipment.

6. Letter of Credit

A bank-related payment mechanism under which a bank undertakes to make payment to the seller subject to specified conditions and documents.

19. World Trade Organization — WTO ⭐

The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an international organisation dealing with the rules of trade between countries.

Major Functions

  • Provides a forum for trade negotiations.
  • Administers international trade agreements.
  • Helps address trade disputes through its established mechanisms.
  • Promotes greater predictability in international trade relations.
  • Provides a framework for discussions on international trade policies.
Exam Point:
WTO is related to international trade and the rules governing trade relations among its members.

20. Case Study Practice 📝

Case 1:

An Indian company sells its products to a buyer in the United Kingdom.

Question: Identify the activity.

Answer: Export trade.
Case 2:

An Indian company purchases machinery from a German supplier.

Question: Identify the activity from the Indian company's perspective.

Answer: Import trade.
Case 3:

Two companies from different countries agree to combine resources and share the risks and returns of a new business project.

Question: Identify the mode of international business.

Answer: Joint venture.
Case 4:

A company allows a foreign business to use its trademark and technology for an agreed fee.

Question: Identify the mode.

Answer: Licensing.
Case 5:

A foreign brand permits a local operator to use its brand, operating system and business procedures.

Question: Identify the mode.

Answer: Franchising.
