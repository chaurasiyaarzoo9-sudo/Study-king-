Chapter 4 — Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand measures the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in one of its determinants.

Chapter Roadmap:
Meaning → Types → Price Elasticity → Percentage Method → Total Expenditure Method → Degrees → Factors → Schedules → Graphs → Numericals → Exam Questions

1. Meaning of Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand refers to the degree to which quantity demanded changes in response to a change in price, income, or the price of related goods.

Main Types

  1. Price Elasticity of Demand
  2. Income Elasticity of Demand
  3. Cross Elasticity of Demand
For Class 11, Price Elasticity of Demand is especially important for numerical questions and diagrams.

2. Price Elasticity of Demand

Price elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of the commodity, other things remaining constant.

Ed = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded ÷ Percentage Change in Price

The numerical value is commonly expressed as an absolute value. In the usual inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded, the algebraic value may be negative.

3. Percentage / Proportionate Method

Ed = (ΔQ / Q) ÷ (ΔP / P)

It can also be written as:

Ed = (ΔQ / ΔP) × (P / Q)

Where:

  • ΔQ = Change in quantity demanded
  • Q = Original quantity demanded
  • ΔP = Change in price
  • P = Original price

4. Solved Numerical — Percentage Method

Price of a commodity falls from ₹10 to ₹8 and quantity demanded increases from 20 units to 25 units. Calculate price elasticity of demand using the proportionate method.

Step 1: Find changes

ΔQ = 25 − 20 = 5
ΔP = 8 − 10 = −2

Step 2: Apply formula

Ed = (ΔQ / ΔP) × (P / Q)

Ed = (5 / −2) × (10 / 20)

Ed = −1.25
In absolute terms, elasticity = 1.25. The negative sign reflects the inverse price-demand relationship.

5. Degrees of Price Elasticity

There are five important cases:

  1. Perfectly Inelastic Demand
  2. Relatively Inelastic Demand
  3. Unitary Elastic Demand
  4. Relatively Elastic Demand
  5. Perfectly Elastic Demand

6. Perfectly Inelastic Demand

When quantity demanded does not change at all despite a change in price, demand is perfectly inelastic.

Ed = 0
Perfectly Inelastic Demand Curve
The curve is vertical because quantity remains unchanged.

7. Relatively Inelastic Demand

When percentage change in quantity demanded is less than the percentage change in price, demand is relatively inelastic.

0 < Ed < 1
Relatively Inelastic Demand

8. Unitary Elastic Demand

When percentage change in quantity demanded is exactly equal to percentage change in price, demand is unitary elastic.

Ed = 1
Unitary Elastic Demand

9. Relatively Elastic Demand

When percentage change in quantity demanded is greater than the percentage change in price, demand is relatively elastic.

Ed > 1
Relatively Elastic Demand

10. Perfectly Elastic Demand

When an extremely small change in price causes quantity demanded to change without limit in the theoretical model, demand is perfectly elastic.

Ed = ∞
Perfectly Elastic Demand Curve

11. Summary of Degrees

Type Elasticity Curve
Perfectly Inelastic Ed = 0 Vertical
Relatively Inelastic 0 < Ed < 1 Steep
Unitary Elastic Ed = 1 Rectangular Hyperbola
Relatively Elastic Ed > 1 Flatter
Perfectly Elastic Ed = ∞ Horizontal

12. Total Expenditure Method

Under the total expenditure method, elasticity of demand is determined by observing the change in total expenditure when price changes.

Total Expenditure = Price × Quantity Demanded

Case 1: Elastic Demand

If price falls and total expenditure rises, demand is elastic. Similarly, if price rises and total expenditure falls, demand is elastic.

Case 2: Inelastic Demand

If price falls and total expenditure falls, demand is inelastic. Similarly, if price rises and total expenditure rises, demand is inelastic.

Case 3: Unitary Elastic

If price changes but total expenditure remains unchanged, demand is unitary elastic.

14. Solved Numerical — Total Expenditure

Price falls from ₹10 to ₹8 and quantity demanded increases from 5 units to 7 units.

At Price ₹10

TE = 10 × 5 = ₹50

At Price ₹8

TE = 8 × 7 = ₹56
Price falls and total expenditure rises from ₹50 to ₹56. Therefore, demand is elastic over this change.

15. Factors Affecting Price Elasticity

  1. Availability of substitutes: More substitutes generally make demand more elastic.
  2. Nature of commodity: Necessities tend to have less elastic demand than many luxuries.
  3. Proportion of income: Goods taking a larger share of income may have more elastic demand.
  4. Time period: Demand may become more elastic over a longer period as consumers get more opportunities to adjust.
  5. Number of uses: Goods having several uses may have relatively elastic demand.

16. Income Elasticity of Demand

Income elasticity measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in consumer income.

Ey = Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded ÷ Percentage Change in Income

Interpretation

  • Positive for many normal goods.
  • Negative for many inferior goods.

17. Cross Elasticity of Demand

Cross elasticity measures the responsiveness of demand for one commodity to a change in the price of another commodity.

Exy = Percentage Change in Demand for X ÷ Percentage Change in Price of Y

Substitutes

Cross elasticity is generally positive for substitute goods.

Example: Price of tea ↑ → Demand for coffee may ↑

Complements

Cross elasticity is generally negative for complementary goods.

Example: Price of petrol ↑ → Demand for cars may ↓

19. Quick Numerical Practice

Question 1

Price rises from ₹5 to ₹6 and quantity demanded falls from 20 units to 16 units. Find the proportionate-method elasticity.

ΔQ = 16 − 20 = −4
ΔP = 6 − 5 = 1

Ed = (−4 / 1) × (5 / 20)
Ed = −1

Absolute Ed = 1

Question 2

Price = ₹20 and quantity = 10 units. Price falls to ₹16 and quantity rises to 15 units. Calculate the new total expenditure.

New TE = 16 × 15 = ₹240
