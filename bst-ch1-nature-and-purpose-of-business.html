📖 Chapter 1 — Business, Trade and Commerce

Business is an economic activity concerned with the production, purchase, sale or exchange of goods and services with the objective of earning profit and satisfying human wants.

Chapter Roadmap:
Business → Economic Activities → Characteristics of Business → Business, Profession & Employment → Objectives → Classification of Business Activities → Industry → Commerce → Trade → Auxiliaries to Trade → Business Risk → Causes of Risk → Examples → Exam Questions

1. Meaning of Business

The term business refers to an economic activity involving regular production, purchase, sale or exchange of goods and services for the purpose of earning income or profit.

Example:
A person purchases mobile phones from a wholesaler and regularly sells them to customers. This is a business activity because it is done regularly with an intention to earn income.
A single casual sale of a personal item does not automatically become a business activity.

2. Economic Activities

Economic activities are activities undertaken with the objective of earning livelihood or income.

Main Types

  • Business
  • Profession
  • Employment

3. Business as an Economic Activity

Business is considered an economic activity because it is carried out mainly to earn income or profit.

Example:
A baker prepares cakes and sells them to customers regularly. The activity generates income and is therefore economic in nature.

4. Characteristics of Business

1. Economic Activity

Business is an economic activity because it is undertaken to earn income or livelihood.

2. Production or Procurement of Goods and Services

A business either produces goods/services or obtains them from others for further sale.

A furniture manufacturer produces tables. A retailer purchases those tables and sells them to customers.

3. Sale or Exchange of Goods and Services

Business involves transfer or exchange of goods or services. Goods produced only for personal consumption are not treated as business transactions.

4. Regular Dealings

Business involves regularity. A one-time transaction normally does not constitute business.

5. Profit Motive

Profit is an important objective of business because it provides a return to the owner and supports continuity and growth.

6. Uncertainty of Return

The amount of profit cannot be guaranteed because business conditions can change.

7. Element of Risk

Every business faces the possibility of loss due to uncertainty and changing market conditions.

Easy Memory:
Economic activity → Production/Procurement → Sale/Exchange → Regularity → Profit → Uncertainty → Risk

5. Business, Profession and Employment

Business, profession and employment are all economic activities, but they differ in important ways.

Basis Business Profession Employment
Meaning Production/sale of goods or services Specialised service based on expertise Work under an employer
Main Objective Profit Professional income/service Salary or wages
Qualification Generally no fixed professional qualification Specialised qualification usually required Depends on the job
Capital Often required Usually some capital may be needed Generally not required from employee
Risk Generally high Relatively lower business risk Generally borne differently; employee receives agreed remuneration
Return Profit Professional fee Salary/wages

6. Business vs Profession — Example

Business: A person owns a clothing store and earns income from selling clothes.

Profession: A qualified doctor provides medical services and receives professional fees.

7. Business vs Employment — Example

Business: A person operates a restaurant and bears the business risk.

Employment: A person works as an accountant in a company and receives salary according to the employment agreement.

8. Objectives of Business

Profit is important, but modern businesses generally pursue several economic and social objectives.

A. Economic Objectives

  • Earning profit
  • Survival
  • Growth
  • Expansion
  • Market standing

B. Social Objectives

  • Supply quality goods and services
  • Charge fair and reasonable prices
  • Create employment opportunities
  • Protect consumers
  • Use resources responsibly
  • Contribute to social welfare
Example:
A company may aim to earn profit while also providing safe products, creating jobs and reducing waste.

9. Classification of Business Activities

Business activities are broadly classified into:

10. Industry

Industry refers to activities concerned with production, processing or extraction of goods.

Main Types of Industry

  • Primary industry
  • Secondary industry
  • Tertiary industry

11. Primary Industry

Primary industries are concerned with extraction and production of natural resources.

Types

  • Extractive industries
  • Genetic industries

Extractive Industry

These industries extract products from natural resources.

Examples: mining, fishing, forestry and oil extraction.

Genetic Industry

These industries involve breeding and multiplication of plants and animals for economic purposes.

Examples: plant nurseries, cattle breeding and poultry farming.

12. Secondary Industry

Secondary industries process raw materials or construct infrastructure.

Manufacturing Industries

Manufacturing industries convert raw materials into finished or semi-finished products.

Cotton → Yarn → Fabric → Clothes

Construction Industry

Construction industries are involved in building infrastructure such as roads, bridges, houses and buildings.

13. Tertiary Industry

Tertiary activities provide services that support production, distribution and consumption.

Examples include banking, insurance, transportation, warehousing, communication and advertising.

14. Commerce

Commerce includes activities involved in the distribution of goods and services from producers to consumers.

It includes:

  • Trade
  • Auxiliaries to trade
Commerce = Trade + Auxiliaries to Trade

15. Trade

Trade refers to buying and selling of goods and services.

Types of Trade

  • Internal Trade
  • External Trade

16. Internal Trade

Internal trade takes place within the geographical boundaries of a country.

Types

  • Wholesale Trade
  • Retail Trade
A wholesaler in Uttar Pradesh sells goods to retailers in Delhi. This is internal trade because the transaction occurs within India.

17. External Trade

External trade takes place between buyers and sellers located in different countries.

Types

  • Import Trade
  • Export Trade
  • Entrepot Trade

18. Import Trade

Import trade means purchasing goods or services from another country for use or sale in the domestic country.

India purchases a particular product from Japan. For India, this is import trade.

19. Export Trade

Export trade means selling goods or services from the domestic country to another country.

An Indian company sells goods to a customer in France. For India, this is export trade.

20. Entrepot Trade

Entrepot trade involves importing goods from one country and then exporting those goods to another country.

Country A → Import → Country B → Re-export → Country C
The key idea is import followed by re-export.

21. Auxiliaries to Trade

Auxiliaries to trade are activities that support the smooth movement of goods and services from producers to consumers.

  • Transport
  • Warehousing
  • Banking and finance
  • Insurance
  • Communication
  • Advertising

22. Transport

Transport removes the geographical distance between producers and consumers.

A manufacturer in Gujarat can send goods to a retailer in Uttar Pradesh through road, rail, air or other transport systems.

23. Warehousing

Warehousing provides storage facilities and helps bridge the time gap between production and consumption.

A manufacturer produces winter jackets months before the winter season. Warehousing allows the jackets to be stored until demand increases.

24. Banking and Finance

Businesses need funds for starting, operating and expanding their activities. Banks and other financial institutions facilitate payments, credit and financial services.

A business obtains working-capital finance from a bank to purchase raw materials.

25. Insurance

Insurance provides financial protection against specified risks subject to the terms of the insurance contract.

A business may insure its property against specified risks. If a covered event occurs, compensation may be available according to the policy terms.

26. Communication

Communication enables businesses to exchange information with customers, suppliers, employees and other stakeholders.

Email, telephone, websites and digital communication help a business communicate with customers and suppliers.

27. Advertising

Advertising informs potential customers about products, services, features and availability.

A company launches a new product and uses online advertisements to inform consumers about it.

28. How Auxiliaries Remove Hindrances

Hindrance Auxiliary How It Helps
Place Transport Moves goods
Time Warehousing Stores goods
Finance Banking Provides financial services
Risk Insurance Provides financial protection against specified risks
Information Communication Transfers information
Promotion Advertising Creates product awareness

29. Business Risk

Business risk refers to the possibility of inadequate profits or even losses because of uncertainties associated with business operations.

Business Risk = Possibility of Loss / Inadequate Return
Important: Risk is an inherent part of business. Profit is not guaranteed.

30. Causes of Business Risk

1. Natural Causes

Floods, earthquakes, storms, droughts and other natural events may affect business.

2. Human Causes

Accidents, theft, strikes, negligence and similar events can create business risk.

3. Economic Causes

Changes in demand, prices, interest rates, competition and economic conditions can affect business.

4. Other Causes

Government policies, technological changes and other unforeseen events can create uncertainty.

31. Nature of Business Risk

  • Business risk arises due to uncertainty.
  • Risk is an essential feature of business.
  • Risk varies from business to business.
  • Profit is the reward associated with bearing business risk.
  • Risk can be reduced but generally cannot be completely eliminated.
A restaurant may face the risk of changing customer preferences, rising ingredient costs and new competitors.

32. Business Risk — Case Study

Ravi opens a small clothing shop. After six months, a new shopping complex opens nearby and several competitors enter the area. Customer traffic falls and Ravi's sales decrease.

Question: What caused the business risk?

Answer: The business faced competitive and market uncertainty. Changes in competition and customer demand affected its sales.

37. Important Terms 🧠

  • Business: Regular economic activity involving goods/services.
  • Profit: Return remaining after business costs are covered.
  • Industry: Production, processing or extraction activities.
  • Commerce: Trade plus auxiliaries to trade.
  • Trade: Buying and selling of goods/services.
  • Import: Purchase from another country.
  • Export: Sale to another country.
  • Entrepot: Import followed by re-export.
  • Business Risk: Possibility of inadequate return or loss.

38. Case Study Practice ⭐

Case 1

A person purchases 100 notebooks and regularly sells them to students for earning income.

Answer: It is a business activity because there is regular dealing in goods with an intention to earn income.

Case 2

A qualified lawyer provides legal services to clients and receives professional fees.

Answer: This is a profession because it requires specialised knowledge and professional expertise.

Case 3

A person works in a company and receives a fixed monthly salary.

Answer: This is employment because the person works under an employer for remuneration.

Case 4

A trader purchases goods from another country and sells them in India.

Answer: This is import trade from the perspective of India.

Case 5

A business stores goods in a warehouse until they are required by customers.

Answer: Warehousing removes the time gap between production and consumption.
