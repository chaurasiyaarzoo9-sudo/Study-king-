Chapter 1 — Introduction to Micro Economics

This chapter introduces the basic ideas of economics, scarcity, choice, opportunity cost and the difference between microeconomics and macroeconomics.

1. Meaning of Economics

Economics is the study of how people and society use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants.

Key idea: Human wants are unlimited, but resources available to satisfy those wants are limited.

2. Scarcity of Resources

Resources such as land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship are limited in relation to human wants. This condition is known as scarcity.

Examples:

  • Limited money
  • Limited time
  • Limited natural resources
  • Limited labour and capital

3. Choice

Because resources are scarce, people have to choose between different alternatives.

Choice means selecting one alternative from the available alternatives.

4. Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that is sacrificed when a choice is made.

Opportunity Cost = Value of Next Best Alternative Forgone

Example:

If you spend your evening studying instead of playing, the opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative you gave up.

5. Economic Problem

The basic economic problem arises because resources are scarce and have alternative uses, while human wants are unlimited.

Three basic economic questions:

  1. What to produce?
  2. How to produce?
  3. For whom to produce?

6. Microeconomics

Microeconomics studies the behaviour of individual consumers, producers and individual markets.

Examples:

  • Demand of a consumer
  • Price of a particular commodity
  • Production of a firm
  • Consumer equilibrium

7. Macroeconomics

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole and deals with broad economic variables.

Examples:

  • National income
  • Inflation
  • Unemployment
  • Economic growth

8. Positive Economics

Positive economics deals with facts and statements that can be tested or verified.

Example: An increase in price may reduce the quantity demanded, other things remaining constant.

9. Normative Economics

Normative economics deals with value judgments and statements about what ought to be.

Example: The government should reduce taxes on essential goods.

10. Central Problems of an Economy

  • What to produce?
  • How much to produce?
  • How to produce?
  • For whom to produce?
