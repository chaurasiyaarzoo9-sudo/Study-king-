Chapter 3 — Public, Private and Global Enterprises

📚 Chapter Overview

Business enterprises can be organised and owned in different ways. One important basis of classification is the ownership of the enterprise. On this basis, enterprises can broadly be divided into the private sector and the public sector.

This chapter covers:

Private Sector Public Sector Departmental Undertaking Statutory Corporation Government Company Global Enterprise Joint Venture PPP

1. Private Sector Enterprises

Private sector enterprises are businesses that are owned and controlled by private individuals or private organisations rather than directly owned by the government.

Example:
A privately owned manufacturing company, retail business or technology company operating for business purposes is part of the private sector.

Main Characteristics

  • Private ownership
  • Profit-oriented operations
  • Management by private owners or appointed managers
  • Decision-making is generally faster
  • Competition plays an important role

2. Public Sector Enterprises

Public sector enterprises are enterprises in which the government has ownership and control according to the form of organisation and applicable law.

Public Sector = Government ownership/control

Public enterprises may be established to provide important goods and services, develop infrastructure and pursue broader economic and social objectives along with commercial objectives.

3. Forms of Public Sector Enterprises

There are three major forms:

  1. Departmental Undertaking
  2. Statutory Corporation
  3. Government Company
Departmental Undertaking ↓ Statutory Corporation ↓ Government Company

4. Departmental Undertaking

A departmental undertaking is an enterprise that operates as a part of a government department and is directly controlled by the concerned government ministry or department.

Example:
Postal services are traditionally discussed in textbooks as an example of a departmental form of public enterprise.

Features

  • It is directly controlled by a government department.
  • It is subject to government rules and procedures.
  • Employees are generally government employees.
  • Its budget is closely connected with the government budget.
  • Government has direct control over its operations.

5. Merits of Departmental Undertaking

  • Direct Government Control: Government can directly supervise operations.
  • Public Accountability: It remains accountable through government mechanisms.
  • Confidentiality: Sensitive activities can be kept under direct government control.
  • Public Interest: Services can be operated with public objectives in mind.

6. Limitations of Departmental Undertaking

  • Excessive government control may reduce flexibility.
  • Decision-making can be slow because of administrative procedures.
  • Political or administrative interference may affect operations.
  • It may have limited commercial flexibility.

7. Statutory Corporation

A statutory corporation is a public enterprise created by a special Act of Parliament or a State Legislature.

Key Point:
Its powers, functions and conditions of operation are defined by the specific law through which it is created.

Features

  • Created by a special Act.
  • Has a separate legal identity.
  • Usually has greater operational autonomy than a departmental undertaking.
  • Government generally exercises overall control.
  • Its powers and functions are defined by the relevant statute.

8. Merits of Statutory Corporation

  • Autonomy: It can operate with greater independence within its legal framework.
  • Flexibility: Management may have more flexibility than a department.
  • Public Accountability: It remains accountable through the law and government oversight.
  • Service Orientation: It can focus on important public services.

9. Limitations of Statutory Corporation

  • Changes in the governing law may require legislative action.
  • Government influence can affect decision-making.
  • Autonomy may vary depending on the statute and government control.
  • Operational efficiency can be affected by administrative constraints.

10. Government Company

A government company is a company in which at least 51% of the paid-up share capital is held by the Central Government, State Government(s), or jointly by the Central and State Governments, as provided under the Companies Act.

Exam Point ⭐
Remember: Government company → minimum 51% government shareholding.

Features

  • It is incorporated under company law.
  • It has a separate legal identity.
  • Government holds at least 51% of paid-up share capital.
  • It can have greater managerial flexibility than a departmental undertaking.
  • Government exercises significant ownership and control.

11. Merits of Government Company

  • Greater operational flexibility.
  • Separate legal identity.
  • Can combine government ownership with corporate management.
  • Can raise funds through permitted corporate mechanisms.
  • Professional management can be used.

12. Limitations of Government Company

  • Government control may influence management decisions.
  • Political or administrative considerations can affect operations.
  • Changes in government policies can affect the enterprise.

14. Global Enterprises 🌎

Global enterprises, commonly called multinational corporations (MNCs), are enterprises that operate in more than one country and coordinate business activities across national boundaries.

Example:
A company may have its headquarters in one country, manufacturing facilities in another country and sales operations across several countries.

15. Features of Global Enterprises

1. Huge Capital Resources

Large global enterprises may have substantial financial resources and access to international capital markets.

2. International Operations

They operate across national boundaries through subsidiaries, branches, production facilities, partnerships or other arrangements.

3. Advanced Technology

They may use advanced production, communication and management technologies.

4. Product Innovation

They often invest in research and development and introduce new products and processes.

5. Global Network

Different stages of production, sourcing, distribution and marketing may be spread across different countries.

6. Professional Management

They generally use specialised managers and professional systems for managing large-scale operations.

16. Joint Venture

A joint venture is a business arrangement in which two or more parties agree to combine resources and work together for a particular business activity or project while sharing the associated risks, responsibilities and benefits according to their agreement.

Simple Example:
Company A has technology and Company B has strong distribution networks. They create a joint business arrangement to enter a new market.

17. Types of Joint Venture

1. Contractual Joint Venture

Parties cooperate on the basis of a contractual agreement without necessarily creating a separate jointly owned company.

2. Equity-Based Joint Venture

Two or more parties contribute capital to a jointly owned business entity and share ownership according to their agreed arrangement.

18. Benefits of Joint Venture

  • Access to new markets.
  • Sharing of resources.
  • Sharing of business risks.
  • Access to technology or specialised knowledge.
  • Combination of different strengths.
  • Possible economies of scale.

19. Public Private Partnership (PPP)

Public Private Partnership is an arrangement in which a public authority and a private sector entity work together for providing a public asset or service under an agreed framework.

PPP = Public Sector + Private Sector
Example:
A government authority may work with a private company to develop, operate or maintain infrastructure or a public service under a contractual arrangement.

20. Features of PPP

  • Involvement of both public and private sectors.
  • Sharing of responsibilities according to the agreement.
  • Private sector may contribute finance, technology or expertise.
  • Public authority remains responsible for public objectives.
  • Risks may be allocated between the parties according to the contract.
  • Usually based on a long-term contractual relationship.

23. Important Terms 🧠

Public Sector

Sector involving government ownership and/or control.

Private Sector

Sector consisting of privately owned enterprises.

Departmental Undertaking

A public enterprise operated as a government department.

Statutory Corporation

An enterprise created by a special Act.

Government Company

A company with at least 51% government-held paid-up share capital as defined under company law.

Global Enterprise

An enterprise operating across national boundaries.

Joint Venture

A cooperative business arrangement between two or more parties.

PPP

A partnership arrangement between public authorities and private entities for specified public assets/services.

26. Case Study Practice 📝

Case:

A government wants to provide an important service directly under the control of one of its departments. The employees are treated as government employees and the organisation is subject to government administrative procedures.

Question: Which form of public enterprise is being described?

Answer: Departmental Undertaking.
Case:

A public enterprise is created through a special Act. The Act specifies its powers, functions and management structure.

Question: Identify the form.

Answer: Statutory Corporation.
Case:

The government owns 60% of the paid-up share capital of a company. The company has its own legal identity and operates under company law.

Question: Identify the form.

Answer: Government Company.
