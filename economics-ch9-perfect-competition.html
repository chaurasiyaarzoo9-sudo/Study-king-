Chapter 9 — Perfect Competition

Perfect competition is a market structure in which there are a large number of buyers and sellers, the product is homogeneous, firms can freely enter or leave the market, and market participants have perfect knowledge of relevant market conditions.

Chapter Roadmap:
Meaning → Features → Firm & Industry → Price Determination → Demand Curve → Revenue → Market Equilibrium → Equilibrium Price → Equilibrium Quantity → Schedules → Graphs → Numericals → Exam Questions

1. Meaning of Perfect Competition

Perfect competition is an idealised market structure where no individual buyer or seller can influence the market price.

The market price is determined by the interaction of total market demand and total market supply.

Market Demand + Market Supply
↓
Market Price
A firm under perfect competition is called a price taker.

2. Main Features of Perfect Competition

1. Large Number of Buyers and Sellers

There are many buyers and sellers. Each individual buyer or seller is too small relative to the whole market to determine the market price independently.

2. Homogeneous Product

All firms sell identical or homogeneous products in the standard model.

3. Free Entry and Exit

Firms can enter or leave the industry without major restrictions in the idealised model.

4. Perfect Knowledge

Buyers and sellers are assumed to have knowledge of relevant market prices and conditions.

5. Perfect Mobility of Factors

Factors of production are assumed to be able to move between uses without major restrictions.

6. No Individual Price Control

An individual firm accepts the market-determined price.

7. Competition

Because products are homogeneous, firms compete mainly through market conditions rather than product differentiation.

3. Firm and Industry

Firm

A firm is an individual production unit that produces and sells a commodity.

Industry

An industry is a collection of firms producing the same or closely related commodity under the market definition being used.

Remember:
Firm = Individual seller/production unit
Industry = Group of firms

4. Price Determination

Under perfect competition, price is determined by the interaction of market demand and market supply.

The equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

Qd = Qs

Where:

  • Qd = Quantity demanded
  • Qs = Quantity supplied

At ₹40, quantity demanded = quantity supplied = 50 units. Therefore, ₹40 is the equilibrium price in this illustrative schedule.

6. Market Equilibrium

Market equilibrium is the situation in which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at a particular price.

Equilibrium Condition:

Qd = Qs

The corresponding price is called the equilibrium price and the corresponding quantity is called the equilibrium quantity.

Equilibrium Price = Price at Qd = Qs

Equilibrium Quantity = Quantity at Qd = Qs

7. Equilibrium Price and Quantity Graph

E = Equilibrium point
Pe = Equilibrium price
Qe = Equilibrium quantity

8. Excess Demand

Excess demand occurs when quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at the prevailing price.

Qd > Qs

→ Excess Demand

In the standard competitive adjustment model, excess demand creates upward pressure on price.

Example

At ₹20:

Qd = 70
Qs = 30
Excess Demand = 70 − 30 = 40 units

9. Excess Supply

Excess supply occurs when quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at the prevailing price.

Qs > Qd

→ Excess Supply

In the standard competitive adjustment model, excess supply creates downward pressure on price.

Example

At ₹50:

Qs = 60
Qd = 40
Excess Supply = 60 − 40 = 20 units

10. Adjustment Towards Equilibrium

Situation Condition Pressure on Price
Excess Demand Qd > Qs Upward
Equilibrium Qd = Qs No shortage/surplus
Excess Supply Qs > Qd Downward

11. Firm Under Perfect Competition

An individual competitive firm takes the market price as given. Therefore, it can sell its output at the prevailing market price subject to the market assumptions.

Price = AR = MR

Thus, the firm's average revenue and marginal revenue curves coincide with its price line.

12. Demand Curve of a Competitive Firm

The demand curve facing an individual perfectly competitive firm is horizontal at the market price.

For the individual competitive firm: AR = MR = Price.

13. Why Is the Firm a Price Taker?

  • There are many sellers.
  • The product is homogeneous.
  • Each firm supplies only a small part of total market output.
  • Buyers can switch to other sellers because products are identical.
  • Therefore, an individual firm cannot independently set a different market price in the standard model.

14. Short-Run and Long-Run — Basic Idea

Short Run

In the short run, some factors of production are fixed while others can be varied.

Long Run

In the long run, all factors can be varied and firms can adjust the scale of production. Under the standard model, entry and exit are also possible.

For Class 11 revision, remember: Short run = at least one factor fixed
Long run = all factors variable

15. Profit — Basic Formula

A firm's profit is the difference between total revenue and total cost.

Profit = TR − TC

Where:

  • TR = Total Revenue
  • TC = Total Cost
TR = Price × Quantity

16. Important Revenue Relationships

Concept Formula / Relationship
Total Revenue TR = P × Q
Average Revenue AR = TR ÷ Q
Marginal Revenue MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ
Perfect Competition AR = MR = P

17. Solved Numerical — Equilibrium

Suppose market demand and supply are given by:

Qd = 100 − P

Qs = 20 + P

At equilibrium:

Qd = Qs

Therefore:

100 − P = 20 + P

80 = 2P

P = ₹40

Substitute P = 40:

Q = 100 − 40

Q = 60 units
Equilibrium Price = ₹40
Equilibrium Quantity = 60 units

18. Solved Numerical — Excess Demand

At a price of ₹30:

Qd = 60
Qs = 40

Therefore:

Excess Demand = Qd − Qs

= 60 − 40

= 20 units

19. Solved Numerical — Excess Supply

At a price of ₹50:

Qd = 40
Qs = 60

Therefore:

Excess Supply = Qs − Qd

= 60 − 40

= 20 units

20. Change in Demand and Equilibrium

If demand increases while supply remains unchanged, the equilibrium price and quantity generally rise in the standard demand-supply model.

The exact effect depends on the shapes and positions of demand and supply curves, but an outward demand shift with supply fixed raises both equilibrium price and quantity in the standard case.

21. Change in Supply and Equilibrium

If supply increases while demand remains unchanged, the equilibrium price generally falls and equilibrium quantity rises in the standard demand-supply model.

The exact change depends on the demand and supply conditions.

22. Important Definitions

Price Taker

A firm that accepts the market-determined price.

Equilibrium Price

The price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

Equilibrium Quantity

The quantity bought and sold at equilibrium price.

Excess Demand

A situation where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.

Excess Supply

A situation where quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.

Homogeneous Product

A product that is identical in relevant characteristics across sellers in the standard model.
