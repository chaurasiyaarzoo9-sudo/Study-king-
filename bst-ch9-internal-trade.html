📚 Chapter 9 — Introduction

Internal trade refers to the buying and selling of goods and services within the geographical boundaries of a country.

Simple Meaning:

Buying + Selling
↓
Within the Same Country
↓
Internal Trade
Example:
A wholesaler in Uttar Pradesh purchases goods from a manufacturer in Maharashtra and sells them to retailers in Uttar Pradesh. This is internal trade because the transaction takes place within India.

Wholesale Trade

Wholesale trade involves buying goods in relatively large quantities and selling them mainly to retailers, other businesses or institutional buyers.

Retail Trade

Retail trade involves selling goods and services in relatively small quantities to final consumers for personal use.

2. Meaning of Wholesale Trade

A wholesaler purchases goods in bulk from producers or other suppliers and sells them in smaller lots to retailers or other business buyers.

Example:
A wholesaler purchases 1,000 notebooks from a manufacturer and sells them in smaller quantities to several stationery shops.

3. Services Provided by Wholesalers to Manufacturers ⭐

1. Facilitates Large-Scale Production

Wholesalers purchase goods in bulk, helping manufacturers sell large quantities of products.

2. Provides Storage

Wholesalers maintain stocks and provide storage facilities.

3. Financial Assistance

In some business arrangements, wholesalers may provide financial support or advance payments to suppliers.

4. Market Information

Wholesalers can provide information about market demand, prices and consumer preferences.

5. Risk Bearing

Wholesalers may bear certain risks related to storage, price changes, damage or deterioration of goods, depending on the nature of the products and agreements.

4. Services Provided by Wholesalers to Retailers

  • Availability of goods in required quantities.
  • Variety of products from different producers.
  • Storage facilities.
  • Credit facilities in some cases.
  • Market information.
  • Regular supply of goods.

5. Meaning of Retail Trade ⭐

Retail trade refers to the sale of goods or services in relatively small quantities to final consumers for personal or household use.

Example:
A customer buys one packet of biscuits from a nearby grocery shop. This is retail trade.

7. Itinerant Retailers

Itinerant retailers do not operate from a fixed permanent shop. They move from one place to another to sell goods.

Types

  • Hawkers and Peddlers
  • Market Traders
  • Street Traders
  • Cheap Jacks
Example:
A vegetable seller who moves around different neighbourhoods and sells vegetables directly to consumers is an example of an itinerant retailer.

8. Hawkers and Peddlers

Hawkers and peddlers move from place to place and sell goods directly to consumers.

Examples:
Vegetables, fruits, snacks, household items and other commonly required products.

9. Market Traders

Market traders sell goods in periodic markets such as weekly markets or fairs.

Example:
A seller who sets up a temporary stall in a weekly market every Sunday.

10. Street Traders

Street traders sell commonly used products from locations such as busy streets, markets or transport areas, subject to applicable local rules.

11. Cheap Jacks

Cheap jacks operate temporary shops in different localities and move when they find better business opportunities.

12. Fixed-Shop Retailers

Fixed-shop retailers operate from permanent establishments.

Major Types

  • General stores
  • Speciality shops
  • Street shops
  • Second-hand goods shops
  • Supermarkets
  • Departmental stores

13. General Stores

General stores sell a variety of commonly used household products.

Example:
A neighbourhood shop selling groceries, stationery, personal-care items and other daily-use products.

14. Speciality Shops

Speciality shops concentrate on a particular category or line of products.

Examples:
Shoe shop, mobile phone shop, book shop or furniture shop.

15. Departmental Stores ⭐

A departmental store is a large retail establishment that offers different categories of goods under one organisation, usually through separate departments.

Features

  • Large-scale retail operation.
  • Many product categories.
  • Different departments.
  • Centralised management.
  • Convenience of one-stop shopping.

16. Supermarket ⭐

A supermarket is a large self-service retail store offering a wide range of products, particularly groceries and household goods.

Features

  • Self-service.
  • Wide variety of products.
  • Usually fixed location.
  • Emphasis on volume sales.
  • Customers select products themselves.

17. Departmental Store vs Supermarket

Basis Departmental Store Supermarket
Departments Different specialised departments Usually organised around product categories
Products Very wide range of goods and services Mainly groceries and household products
Service May provide more customer assistance Primarily self-service
Shopping One-stop shopping Convenient self-service shopping

18. Consumer Cooperative Store

A consumer cooperative store is owned and managed by consumers or their cooperative organisation for supplying goods to members and customers.

Objectives

  • Provide goods at reasonable prices.
  • Reduce dependence on intermediaries.
  • Protect consumer interests.
  • Provide quality products.

19. Modern Forms of Retail Trade 💻

Electronic Retailing

Goods and services can be offered to customers through websites, mobile applications and other digital platforms.

Advantages

  • Convenience.
  • Wider market reach.
  • 24-hour access to online platforms.
  • Easy comparison of products and prices.
  • Home delivery options.
Example:
A customer orders a school bag through an online shopping platform and receives it at home.

22. Services of Retailers to Consumers ⭐

  • Provide goods in small quantities.
  • Provide convenience of location.
  • Provide variety of products.
  • Provide product information.
  • May provide credit to regular customers.
  • Provide after-sales services for certain products.
  • Maintain regular availability of goods.

23. Services of Wholesalers to Producers

  • Bulk purchase of goods.
  • Storage.
  • Market information.
  • Financial assistance in certain arrangements.
  • Risk bearing.
  • Distribution support.

24. Important Documents in Internal Trade

Invoice

An invoice is a document showing details of goods sold, quantities, prices and other relevant transaction information.

Cash Memo

A cash memo is a document generally issued for cash sales and records details of the transaction.

Example:
When you purchase stationery from a shop, the seller may provide a cash memo showing the items purchased and amount paid.

25. Case Study Practice 📝

Case 1:

A trader purchases goods in bulk from manufacturers and sells them to several retailers.

Question: Identify the type of trade.

Answer: Wholesale trade.
Case 2:

A person moves through different neighbourhoods selling vegetables directly to consumers.

Question: Identify the type of retailer.

Answer: Hawker/Peddler.
Case 3:

A large store has separate departments for clothing, electronics, household products and other categories.

Question: Identify the type of retail store.

Answer: Departmental store.
Case 4:

Customers select products themselves from shelves and pay at a checkout counter.

Question: Identify the retail format.

Answer: Supermarket / self-service retail format.
