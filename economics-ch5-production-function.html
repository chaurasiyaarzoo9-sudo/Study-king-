Chapter 5 — Production Function

Production is the process of transforming inputs into output. Production function explains the relationship between inputs used and the maximum output that can be produced with those inputs.

Chapter Roadmap:
Production → Factors of Production → Production Function → Short Run → Long Run → TP → AP → MP → Schedules → Graphs → Law of Variable Proportions → Numericals → Exam Questions

1. Meaning of Production

Production is the process of converting inputs into goods and services that satisfy human wants.

Examples

  • A factory converts raw materials into finished goods.
  • A farmer uses land, labour and capital to produce crops.
  • A teacher provides educational services.

2. Factors of Production

1. Land

Land includes all natural resources used in production.

2. Labour

Labour refers to human physical and mental effort used in production.

3. Capital

Capital includes man-made resources used for further production, such as machines, tools and buildings.

4. Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur organises factors of production, takes business decisions and bears risk.

3. Production Function

A production function shows the functional relationship between inputs and maximum output that can be produced with given technology.

Q = f(L, K)

Where:

  • Q = Output
  • L = Labour
  • K = Capital
A production function is concerned with the maximum possible output from given inputs and technology.

4. Short Run and Long Run

Short Run

Short run is a period in which at least one factor of production is fixed while other factors can be varied.

Long Run

Long run is a period in which all factors of production can be varied.

Basis Short Run Long Run
Factors At least one fixed All variable
Plant size Generally fixed Can be changed
Time Relatively shorter Relatively longer

5. Fixed Factor and Variable Factor

Fixed Factor

A factor whose quantity cannot be changed during the short run is called a fixed factor.

Example: Size of factory building in the short run.

Variable Factor

A factor whose quantity can be changed during the short run is called a variable factor.

Example: Labour employed by a firm.

6. Total Product (TP)

Total Product is the total quantity of output produced by a firm with a given quantity of inputs.

TP = Total Output

When one variable factor is increased while other factors remain fixed, TP initially rises and may later fall.

7. Average Product (AP)

Average Product is output produced per unit of the variable factor.

AP = TP ÷ Units of Variable Factor

If labour is the variable factor:

AP = TP ÷ L

8. Marginal Product (MP)

Marginal Product is the change in total product resulting from the use of one additional unit of the variable factor.

MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL
MP tells us the additional output obtained from an additional unit of the variable factor.

10. Relationship Between TP and MP

  • When MP is positive, TP increases.
  • When MP rises, TP increases at an increasing rate.
  • When MP falls but remains positive, TP increases at a decreasing rate.
  • When MP becomes zero, TP is maximum.
  • When MP becomes negative, TP falls.
TP is maximum when MP = 0

11. Relationship Between AP and MP

  • When MP is greater than AP, AP rises.
  • When MP is equal to AP, AP is maximum.
  • When MP is less than AP, AP falls.
MP = AP → AP is maximum
The MP curve intersects the AP curve at the maximum point of the AP curve.

15. Law of Variable Proportions

The Law of Variable Proportions explains the short-run behaviour of output when one factor is varied while other factors remain fixed.

As more units of a variable factor are added to fixed factors, the total product initially increases at an increasing rate, then at a decreasing rate, and eventually may decline.

16. Three Stages of the Law

Stage I — Increasing Returns

  • TP increases at an increasing rate initially.
  • MP rises.
  • Later MP starts falling but remains positive.
  • AP reaches its maximum at the end of Stage I.

Stage II — Diminishing Returns

  • TP increases at a decreasing rate.
  • MP continues to fall but remains positive.
  • TP reaches maximum at the end of Stage II.

Stage III — Negative Returns

  • MP becomes negative.
  • TP starts falling.
  • Additional units of the variable factor reduce output.

17. Three Stages — Summary Table

Stage TP MP AP
Stage I Increasing Initially rises, then falls Rises
Stage II Increasing at decreasing rate Positive but falling Falls
Stage III Falls Negative Falls

18. Rational Stage of Production

A rational producer generally operates in Stage II of the Law of Variable Proportions.

Stage II is considered the economically relevant stage because MP is positive while the fixed factor is being used efficiently.

19. Numerical Example — AP

A firm produces 60 units of output using 5 workers. Find Average Product.

AP = TP ÷ L

AP = 60 ÷ 5

AP = 12 units per worker

20. Numerical Example — MP

Total product increases from 50 units to 65 units when labour increases from 4 workers to 5 workers. Find MP.

MP = ΔTP ÷ ΔL

MP = (65 − 50) ÷ (5 − 4)

MP = 15 units

21. Numerical Example — TP from AP

Average product of 6 workers is 15 units. Find total product.

AP = TP ÷ L

TP = AP × L

TP = 15 × 6

TP = 90 units

22. Important Relationships

Condition Result
MP > AP AP rises
MP = AP AP is maximum
MP < AP AP falls
MP = 0 TP is maximum
MP < 0 TP falls

23. Important Assumptions

  • Technology remains constant.
  • At least one factor is fixed in the short run.
  • Units of the variable factor are homogeneous.
  • Production takes place over a relevant short-run period.
  • Other relevant conditions remain unchanged.

24. Production Function vs Production

Production Production Function
Actual process of producing goods/services Relationship between inputs and maximum output
Describes activity Describes technical relationship
