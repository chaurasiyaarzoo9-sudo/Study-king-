📚 Chapter 5 — Emerging Modes of Business

Technology has changed the way businesses communicate with customers, suppliers, employees and other stakeholders. Businesses can now perform many activities electronically through computers, smartphones and the internet.

Main Concept:

Traditional Business ⬇️
Technology + Internet ⬇️
Emerging Modes of Business
Example:
Buying products through an online platform, paying electronically and receiving the product at home are examples of modern business activities supported by technology.

1. Meaning of E-Business 🌐

E-business means conducting business activities using computer networks, electronic communication and internet-based technologies.

Simple Definition:

E-Business = Business activities conducted electronically.

E-business can cover activities such as buying and selling, customer support, electronic payments, supply-chain coordination, communication and information exchange.

2. E-Commerce vs E-Business

Basis E-Commerce E-Business
Meaning Electronic buying and selling of goods/services Broader use of electronic systems in business activities
Scope Narrower Broader
Activities Ordering, selling, purchasing and payments E-commerce + communication, customer support, supply chain and other activities
Example Online purchase of a product Online sales + electronic customer service + digital supply-chain coordination
Remember ⭐

E-Commerce is mainly concerned with electronic commercial transactions, while E-Business has a wider scope.

3. Scope of E-Business

E-business can be used in many areas of business.

B2B B2C C2C C2B Intra-B

4. B2B — Business to Business

B2B refers to electronic transactions between one business and another business.

Example:
A manufacturer orders raw materials from a supplier through an online business platform.
Business → Business

5. B2C — Business to Consumer

B2C refers to transactions in which a business sells goods or services directly to individual consumers through electronic channels.

Example:
A customer purchases a pair of shoes from an online store.
Business → Consumer

6. C2C — Consumer to Consumer

C2C refers to transactions between individual consumers through an electronic platform.

Example:
One individual sells a used textbook to another individual through an online marketplace.
Consumer → Consumer

7. C2B — Consumer to Business

C2B refers to situations where individuals offer products, services or value to businesses through electronic platforms.

Example:
A freelancer provides a design service to a company through an online platform.
Consumer → Business

8. Intra-B — Within Business

Intra-B commerce refers to electronic communication and transactions within different departments, employees or units of the same organisation.

Example:
A company's sales department shares customer information electronically with its production department.

9. Types of E-Business Transactions ⭐

Type Meaning Example
B2B Business to Business Manufacturer buying raw materials online
B2C Business to Consumer Online retail purchase
C2C Consumer to Consumer Person selling used book online
C2B Consumer to Business Freelancer providing service to company
Intra-B Within the organisation Department-to-department electronic communication

10. Benefits of E-Business ⭐

1. Ease of Formation

Technology can make it easier for some businesses to establish online operations compared with setting up a large physical network.

2. Lower Investment Requirements

An online business may require less investment in physical infrastructure for certain activities.

3. Convenience

Customers can access many online services from locations where internet access is available.

4. Speed

Information, orders and payments can be processed electronically very quickly.

5. Wider Reach

Internet-based business can potentially reach customers in different cities, states and countries.

6. 24×7 Availability

Many online platforms can remain accessible around the clock, subject to maintenance and service availability.

7. Reduced Paperwork

Electronic records and communication can reduce dependence on physical documents for many activities.

8. Global Market Access

Businesses can use digital channels to reach customers and suppliers beyond their local market.

11. Limitations of E-Business ⚠️

1. Lack of Personal Touch

Electronic transactions may provide less direct personal interaction than face-to-face business.

2. Security Concerns

Businesses and customers need to protect accounts, devices and data from cyber risks.

3. Dependence on Technology

Internet connectivity, software and devices are important for many e-business activities.

4. Delivery Issues

Physical products purchased online still need transportation and delivery.

5. Customer Trust

Customers may have concerns about product quality, seller reliability, payment security or return policies.

6. Legal and Regulatory Issues

Businesses operating electronically must comply with applicable laws and regulations.

12. Advantages vs Limitations

Advantages Limitations

Fast communication Less personal interaction

13. Online Transaction Process 🛒

An online transaction generally involves several stages.

Step 1 — Product/Service Selection
Customer searches for a product or service and selects it.
Step 2 — Order Placement
Customer confirms the order and provides required information.
Step 3 — Payment
Payment is completed using an available payment method.
Step 4 — Order Processing
The seller receives and processes the order.
Step 5 — Delivery
The product is delivered to the customer where applicable.
Step 6 — After-Sales Service
Customer support, returns, warranty or other applicable services may follow.

14. Electronic Payment Methods 💳

Electronic transactions may use different payment mechanisms, depending on the business and banking/payment system.

  • Debit cards
  • Credit cards
  • Internet banking
  • UPI-based payments
  • Electronic wallets
  • Other approved electronic payment methods
Safety Tip:
Never share passwords, PINs, OTPs or other confidential authentication information with another person.

15. Traditional Business vs E-Business

Basis Traditional Business E-Business
Medium Physical/offline channels Electronic/digital channels
Market Reach Often limited by physical presence Can potentially reach wider markets
Communication Often physical or face-to-face Electronic communication
Time May require more physical processing Many activities can be processed quickly
Availability Usually linked to business hours/location Many online services can be available 24×7
Physical Infrastructure Often requires physical premises Can reduce dependence on physical infrastructure for some activities

16. Outsourcing

Outsourcing means obtaining certain business activities or services from an external organisation instead of performing them entirely within the business.

Example:
A company may hire an external agency to handle payroll processing, customer support or information-technology services.

17. Business Process Outsourcing — BPO

Business Process Outsourcing means contracting out specific business processes or functions to an external service provider.

Examples

  • Customer support
  • Data processing
  • Payroll processing
  • Back-office operations
  • Technical support

18. Knowledge Process Outsourcing — KPO

Knowledge Process Outsourcing involves outsourcing activities that require specialised knowledge, analytical skills or professional expertise.

Examples:
Research, data analysis, market research and specialised analytical services.

19. BPO vs KPO ⭐

Basis BPO KPO
Meaning Business Process Outsourcing Knowledge Process Outsourcing
Nature Routine/process-oriented activities Knowledge-intensive activities
Skill Requirement Process-specific skills Specialised knowledge and analytical skills
Examples Customer support, data processing Research, analysis, specialised consulting

20. Need for Outsourcing

  • Focus on core business activities.
  • Access to specialised expertise.
  • Potential reduction in operating costs.
  • Improved efficiency in outsourced activities.
  • Access to technology and skilled personnel.
  • Greater flexibility.

21. Risks of Outsourcing

  • Loss of control over some activities.
  • Confidentiality and data-security concerns.
  • Dependence on external service providers.
  • Quality problems if the provider does not meet standards.
  • Possible communication difficulties.

23. Case Study Practice 📝

Case 1:

A manufacturer purchases raw materials from another company through an electronic business platform.

Question: Identify the type of e-business transaction.

Answer: B2B — Business to Business.
Case 2:

A customer buys a mobile phone from an online retailer.

Question: Identify the type of transaction.

Answer: B2C — Business to Consumer.
Case 3:

An individual sells an old textbook to another individual through an online marketplace.

Question: Identify the type.

Answer: C2C — Consumer to Consumer.
Case 4:

A company hires an outside agency to manage its customer-support operations.

Question: Which concept is involved?

Answer: Outsourcing / BPO.
Case 5:

A research organisation provides specialised market analysis to another company.

Question: Which type of outsourcing is more closely associated with such knowledge-intensive work?

Answer: KPO — Knowledge Process Outsourcing.
